### NEW LEADS
1. **WASHINGTON Co IA, Gallup's $5,000 under a red oak.** Burrell, *History of Washington County* (1909) v.1, p.~139-140.
   - George Gallup sold his place in **Clay** Twp and deserted his family. He was later hanged in California and "on the gallows said he had buried five thousand dollars at the foot of a red oak tree in the **northwest corner of section 16 in Iowa** [township]". It was hunted for and never found.
   - **This is a section-level claim.** Iowa Twp, Washington Co, sec 16 NW corner. It needs BLM bounds (not run). D, a strong legend.
2. **CLINTON IA, Tim Bigelow's "mint".** *History of Clinton County* (1879) p.345; Wolfe 1911 p.413.
   - Bigelow's counterfeit-coin "mint" was in a log house / old blacksmith shop "**in Riverside, below where Davis's lime-kiln now stands**", between Lyons and Camanche. Bell-wires ran to it from the slough bridge (by the later railroad bridge) and the Mill Creek bridge. His house was loopholed. Regulators drove him out.
   - **Placeable via a Clinton city plat or Sanborn (Davis lime-kiln).** D.
3. **POWESHIEK IA, "Bogus Grove".** *History of Poweshiek County* (1880) p.313 and p.450; Parker 1911 p.95.
   - A small grove on the **North English River "directly north of Montezuma"** (Scott Twp area) with an old log cabin. Remains found there led settlers to think it was a hideout of the **Fox and Long gang** of horse thieves and counterfeiters. Blacksmith Snook on Bear Creek was called on by the gang. D.
4. **GREEN Co WI, French Lake's buried money.** *History of Green County* (1884) p.1050.
   - French Lake (b. 1807 VA) settled on **sec 7** of the town (the Juda / Jefferson Twp chapter) in 1839. The first night he "buried his money — of which he had plenty" under the turf, with his camp kettle over it, "where his fine residence now stands". It is a pioneer anecdote (probably dug up again). D, colour; the town needs confirming.
5. **PEPIN Co WI, Bogus Creek.** Curtiss-Wedge, *Buffalo & Pepin* (1919) p.1828; Forrester, *Chippewa Valley* (1891).
   - Biles, farming on **Bogus Creek, Pepin Twp** (from 1848), "turned up with his plow tools and bars of metal" from an early counterfeiting gang, "hence the name of this stream". D; a named creek.
6. **ISABELLA Co MI, Delaney boiling spring.** *Portrait & Biographical Album of Isabella County* (1884).
   - On Mr. Delaney's farm, a boiling spring (500 gal/min) is revered as an Indian chiefs' burial place, and "several individuals claim that in its immediate vicinity a large sum of money was buried during the second war with Great Britain". Farm location not extracted ("first homesteaded north of the Indian reservation"). D.
7. **HURON Co MI, Cane the counterfeiter.** *Portrait & Biographical Album of Huron County* (1884): a settler named Cane made bogus dollars (and bills), and once melted his own coins by mistake. Place not extracted. Colour.
8. **BUREAU Co IL, Wade's pot of gold.** Matson, *Reminiscences of Bureau County* (1872) p.~372-373. Wade, a Dover "magician" with a peep-stone, had two Dover merchants dig a hard gravelly knoll in "the bluff near the present site of **Bureau Junction**" for a pot of gold. A hoax-colour tale.
   - Bradsby 1885 ch. XIX: "counterfeiters in their caves", generic.
9. **Colour, no spot:**
