## NEW LEADS (ranked)

1. **FERGUS FALLS, Smith's buried gold (Otter Tail MN).** Mason, *History of Otter Tail County* (1916), about pp.684-686 (archive.org mn-otter-tail-1916-mason-1).
   - "An eccentric Scotchman, named Smith" lived at Joseph Whitford's cabin (the first settler, 1857), and Smith "was killed by the Indians". A "wild tale of his buried gold on the townsite of Fergus" is reported, and "recently a solitary gold sovereign has been found near the site of the old cabin". The quoted writer mocks the tale.
   - **Cabin site:** "the cellar of his cabin may yet be seen near Whitford street, just inside the fence of Henry G. Page's block."
   - **Pin: Whitford St, downtown Fergus Falls, about 46.2776,-96.0706 (street centroid), r300, D.** Urban, look-only. A Sanborn or plat search for "H. G. Page" would tighten it.
2. **SAUK CO WI, "Bogus Camp", sec 33, Town of Franklin.** *History of Sauk County* (1880), p.409 (historyofsaukcou00west).
   - It sits in a rock pocket under high bluffs, with a grove of oaks and maples and a spring. In 1858 a visitor found a 30 ft water trough into a decayed log cabin, a forge, and a tree-press (a felled maple grooved for dies, plus an oak mortised for a 17 ft red-elm lever). An old pistol had been found there earlier. The gang dated from about the 1830s.
   - **Sec 33 T10N R4E (BLM) = 43.2931-43.3076 N, -90.0342 to -90.0146 W. Pin: sec 33 centre, about 43.300,-90.024, r800, D.** A lidar or air-photo check for the bluff pocket and spring could tighten it.
   - Same volume p.579: counterfeiters **Baxter & Reynolds** passed bogus coin at Reedsburg (1850s) and were caught asleep by a fire under an oak, S part of Woodland town. Colour only.
3. **JACKSON CO IA, counterfeiters' cave on Pine Run, Brandon Twp.** Ellis, *History of Jackson County* (1910) v.1, p.342-343 (historyofjackson01elli_0).
   - "Isaiah Cooley ... discovered the counterfeiters' cave on Pine Run in Brandon township, in 1856-57."
   - Same page: a man vanished in 1840 from the Emeline corners (the Ewing Gilmore place), thought killed "for the money he was supposed to have".
   - p.~300 "Counterfeiter Redeemed by Patriotism": the Brandon/Monmouth border was "infested with an organized band of counterfeiters"; E. S. Washburn was arrested in 1858 with dies.
   - **Emeline = 42.155,-90.831 (Brandon Twp).** Pine Run is not in OSM. **Not pinned:** Pine Run needs a 1890s-1910s Jackson plat (Ancestry 1127) or a USGS name check.
4. **HOUSTON CO MN, cave with a gold coin, Black Hammer Twp.** Neill, *History of Houston County* (1882), p.326 (repeated in the 1919 Curtiss-Wedge).
   - In the N part of the township near Riceford Creek, by the residences of William and Peter Carrier: earthwork "fortifications" and a cave "that may have been formed or modified by human hands", with a door-like 4 ft square entrance, about 1,000 ft long.
   - **A gold coin "about the size of a $5.00 piece" with no intelligible inscription was found in it by Peter C. Carrier** and sold to James Vincent of Houston village.
   - **Not pinned:** the Carrier farms need the 1896 Houston plat (umedia-plats has the Black Hammer plate) or Ancestry 1127.
5. **OSSIAN IA, counterfeiters' rendezvous.** Sparks, *History of Winneshiek County* (1877), pp.130-132.
   - The early Ossian town site on the Military Road (about 1850s): "counterfeiters made it their rendezvous"; "counterfeit gold pieces were in circulation". No one was ever punished.
   - **Pin: Ossian village, 43.146,-91.765, r800, D** (colour; no spot named).
6. **SARANAC MI (Ionia), "A Bogus Band".** *History of Ionia and Montcalm Counties* (1881), about p.206-207.
   - Before 1857, bogus Mexican dollars were made secretly in the village; it came out in 1857 when housebreakers confessed. No site given. **Pin: Saranac village 42.929,-85.213, D (colour).**
7. **LYONS MI (Ionia), counterfeiters' den 1835-36.** Same volume, p.240.
   - A den was found near Lyons in 1835-36. The gang fled and "their tools, dies, etc., were afterwards found in the river" (Grand River). No site. Colour, D.
8. **CLAYTON CO IA.** Price, *History of Clayton County* (1916) v.1:
   - Eliphalet Price letter, end of ch. XVIII (page number not in the OCR): the first blacksmith shop in the county (fall 1834, N side of the Turkey River) belonged to **William Morris, a maker of bogus 50-cent coin**.
   - Early-settlement chapter (page number not in the OCR): "Sodom and Gomorrah", two saloons near present Hardin, **Monona Twp**, area "supposed to harbor a nest of counterfeiters".
   - Colour, D. No spot.
9. **DUBUQUE.** Oldt (1911), p.~148: "A complete set of counterfeiters' tools was unearthed at White and Thirteenth streets" (Oct 1863), thought to be from a gang convicted in 1859. Urban colour, D.
