## 1-2. Larson, Jennings (Lake Twp, Missaukee Co MI)
- **MI death record (coll. 60872, record 3506908, file no. 36): Jens Larson, d. 9 Jan 1916, Lake Twp, Missaukee; age 56; b. 13 Mar 1859, "Danmark"; widowed.** The index shows no occupation or informant (the certificate image is paid; not opened).
- **This Jens does NOT fit the 1923 hermit well.** The linked 1900 census record is Jens S. Larson, 41, b. Mar 1859 Denmark, immigrated 1883, a farmer owning his farm in **Gordon Twp, Todd Co MN**, with wife Hellene (m. 1892) and children **Loy (b. about 1895)**, Roy (about 1897) and Anna (about 1899). Loy Larson is in Lake Twp in 1920 (b. about 1896 IA per that index; a small mismatch). So Jens came to Lake Twp after 1900 as a family farmer, was widowed, and died in 1916. He was Danish, not Swedish, and had sons Loy and Roy, not "John Jr."
- **1910 census, Lake Twp, Missaukee (index):** the only Larsons are **Lewis Larson (b. 1851 Sweden, head) and wife Matilda (b. 1861 Sweden)**, plus Marie Jorgensen / Anne Marie Larsen (b. 1866 Denmark, wife of Fredric P. Jorgensen). There is no John or Jens Larson in Lake Twp in 1910 and no John Larson in Missaukee in 1900 or 1910.
- 1906 plat: L. G. Larson 40, E part sec 24 (see list15.md).
- MI death index: no Lewis Larson in Missaukee (1867-1952). The only Lake Twp Larson death is Jens (1916). 1920 Lake Twp: Loy Larson (b. 1896, Jens's son), wife Vera, son Russell.
- **Reading:** the 1923 story's "John Larson", a Swedish woodcutter-hermit near an outlying lake, may be **Lewis Larson** (Swedish, in Lake Twp, of the right age) or someone missed by the index; Jens (Danish farmer) is a weaker match. Not resolved. Next steps: Lewis Larson's death (MI 1910-23) and the 1920 Lake Twp census.

Highland Park on the Lake Michigan side has the hotel, pavilion, dance hall and "Beach Park". **No "Five Mile Hill" label, no lighthouse reservation and no road or lot labels on the dune.** Viewer p.11 is the downtown/central part (Washington, Franklin, Clinton, Central Park, City Hall).
- **CORRECTION after a closer read (06:18Z):** the grid is **"Storrs & Company's Addition"** (not "Forrest"), and it lies between the river branches. The compass rose shows N up.
  - (a) The hachured dune is drawn **across the "GRAND RIVER" channel from the addition, to its W and NW**, so it is on the **opposite (N/W) side of the main channel**, not S of downtown. This fits **Dewey Hill** (N side) rather than Five Mile Hill.
  - (b) **No streets end at its foot.** River St runs along the addition's river edge facing the dune across the water.
  - (c) **Highland Park / Beach Park is a separate inset** (boxed, to the W, across a frame line); it does not touch the dune.
  - (d) **"Mill Reservation" and "South Channel"** are at the **S edge of the Storrs addition**, not S of the dune.
  - So this plate does **not** show the S-of-channel Five Mile Hill dune. The dune W of Washington/Franklin and E of Highland Park would be on the Middle/East plates (viewer pp.8-9, 11), where no hachured dune was seen at overview zoom. Earlier line withdrawn.

## 14. GB "Trombler", Fergus Falls 1903-05: FOUND (Norwegian-language)
- **Fergus Falls Ugeblad, Thu 1 Oct 1903, p.4** (Norwegian; another hit 5 Nov 1903 not opened). The story of **Wm. Trombler**:
  - He and his brother lived as bachelors in their home on **Mill Street**; the brother (possibly named Edwin) died two years earlier, and Wm. lived alone since. He came up from **Red Wing**.
  - He was a Civil War veteran (a badge showed he belonged to the **14th Minnesota Regiment**). He was found in a disabled state, was taken to the hospital and died (reported Saturday).
  - **J. A. Croft and E. S. Lambert**, with Mr. **McKinstry**, were appointed to inventory the estate; they "soon discovered considerable treasures":
    - a pocketbook on a stand with about **$87** in cash and a draft on a bank for **$200**;
    - "**an old trunk in a corner held $400 in gold**";
    - a memorandum book with about **$200**;
    - 2-3 more pocketbooks with various sums, and **some dollars found in tobacco pouches**.
  - "**In all about $2,300 has been found, and the appraisers are still searching for more.**" (Following line: "the money is being …", cut off.)
  - The article also notes no money was found on the body except small change.
- **Follow-up, Fergus Falls Ugeblad, Thu 5 Nov 1903, p.3:** "The heirs of the late Wm. Trombler, after whom **money was found in all the corners** when he died, now believe that **there must be much more money** after him than has been found so far. According to **pension certificates** found among his papers, he received at least **$15,000 in pension** from the government, and they do not think he spent more than $100 a year. **They believe there must be at least $8,000 to $10,000 that has not yet been found.** The majority of the heirs are also against **J. L. McKinstry** being [administrator] …" (the rest is cut off in view).
- **This makes Trombler a live "unfound" lead: heirs estimated $8,000-10,000 still hidden (Nov 1903), on top of about $2,300 found.**
- This **confirms the cash-in-the-house hoard at the Mill St home** (house no. 11 per the 1900 census, see trombler-jacobson-otter-tail.md). No mention of anything left unfound beyond "still searching".
