# Rudolph Hank (Hanks/Hauks), Kenosha Co. WI (rope maker; d. in asylum Oct 1895): probate (Ancestry, 2026-11-01)

**Collection:** *Wisconsin, U.S., Wills and Probate Records, 1800-1987* (coll. 9088), Kenosha Co., "Probate Case Files, File No 2123 - File No 2166".
- **Case no. 2133**; Ancestry record 264654; 183 images, starting at image 007710085_01119. Index probate date 18 Sep 1896. Viewed only.

## Final account (images 01121-01125; filed 18 Sep 1896)
- **Administrator: John Scharding.**
- **Charges:**
  - 10 Dec 1895: to inventory of personal property, **$15.00**
  - Gain on property sold: $3.38
  - **9 May 1896: to sale of real estate, $225.00**
  - **Total received $243.38**
- **Paid out:**
  - publication notices $18.40; appraisers $3.00; repairs to property for sale $5.00; taxes $1.15; attorney $35.00; 5% commission $12.15; per diem $10.00
  - funeral: coffin express and telegrams $23.18; lot and grave $4.50; hearse $6.00
  - guardian's expenses: groceries $13.00, attorney's fee for guardianship $15.00, etc.
- **Petition for final hearing** (image 01123, sworn 17 Sep 1896): the administrator "has fully administered the estate ... **there remains no residue of said estate, either real estate or personal property**". There is no widow and no children under seven in the state.
- **Land appraisal** (image 01143, 7 Apr 1896): Charles M. Lilley, C. J. Gronquist and E. D. Goodman were sworn to appraise "the land of said Rudolph Hank, deceased, licensed to be sold by the County Court". The land description is in the sale papers (images 10-20 / 28-66); **not read**.
- **Testimony** (image 01140, 4 Nov 1896, County Judge Anthony Van Wyck?): mentions real estate and heirs living out of the county, "about 12 or 13 years" ago; mostly illegible.

## Report of sale (images 01129-01133; filed 23 May 1896)
- Order to sell 7 Apr 1896; appraised 17 Apr; notice in the *Telegraph Courier*. The public auction got no bid above the appraisal.
- **Private sale on 22 May 1896, for $225, to Alphons Savinski** [spelling uncertain].
- **The land** (verbatim in substance): "the tract of land occupied by said deceased as a **homestead** during his life time, situated in the **north-east quarter of section 7, town 1, range 23**, in the County of Kenosha ..., commencing on the east side of the present traveled road through said quarter section, on the south line of lands heretofore conveyed by John G. Freeman to one Mary West, by deed dated June 5th 1851 and recorded in ... Vol. 'A' on page 175; thence east 16 rods; thence north 5 rods; thence west 16 rods to the east line of said road; thence south 5 rods to the place of beginning, **containing one half acre of ground, on which is situated a small dwelling house**, and being all the real estate owned by said deceased."
- **Placement:** a ½-acre lot (16 × 5 rods) on the **E side of the N-S road through the NE¼ of sec 7, T1N R23E** (town of Pleasant Prairie, S of Kenosha city). BLM sec 7 runs -87.8354 to -87.8154, 42.5511 to 42.5659. The NE¼ is about -87.8254 to -87.8154, 42.5585 to 42.5659.
- The exact spot along the road is not known without the Freeman/West deed line. **Suggested pin: NE¼ sec 7 centre, about 42.5622, -87.8204, r400**, refined to the road's E side if the old road is identified (today's road through that quarter). Confidence D/C.

## Significance
- **No money found.** The inventory lists only $15 of personal property. The estate's only asset was land, sold for $225, and the account closed with **no residue**.
- The "$300 plus savings" the guardian hoped to find **never entered the estate**. Either it was not found, or it was spent on guardianship before his death.
- **To place his lot:** read the sale papers (Kenosha case 2133, images 10-20, 28-66) for the legal description.
