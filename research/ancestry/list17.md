## 12. GB "Baty treasure", Michigan 1899-1901: 12 hits; KEY ARTICLE READ
- Hits: Grand Rapids Herald 9-10 Oct 1899 ("Gold at the Haven: Two Strangers Are Trying to Find It"; "Fortune in Yellow: Singular Search for Hidden Gold at Grand Haven"); Jackson Citizen 10 and 13 Oct 1899 (same story). **Grand Rapids Herald, Mon 23 Apr 1900, p.2: "Gold and Diamonds Repose under Five-Mile Hill at Grand Haven / Story of Buried Treasure / Prospectors Are Digging Deep into the Sand / Copy of Grand Rapids Eagle Struck at Depth of 20 Feet".** It is new to research/ (PC a has only the Oct 1899 Baty outcome).
  - "The old **Five Mile hill range on the lake shore, south of Grand Haven**, has a mysterious history of robbers' flight and buried treasure. For 40 years or more…"
  - **Prisoner's story:** "About 40 years ago **Curtis W. Gray**, then an officer of this place, was called to **Jackson** to get a confession from a dying prisoner." The man had belonged to a gang of robbers who made "an immense haul at a bank somewhere in the state". Pursued, they came "within a few miles of the mouth of the Grand" and split up. His share was "mostly of **gold and diamonds**". Officers struck the village, so "in the early morning he quietly took his burden and started for the wooded hills near the lake. He came to **the place where the light house stands**, then turned into the woods, and going a short distance, he could not tell how far, found a suitable spot on **the top of a little ridge of sandy soil**. He made a **chart** of the spot and buried his wealth, carefully covering all marks with brush. He carefully described the spot as **near the light house, overlooking the lake and river**, and many miles of back country." He was later imprisoned for another crime, came back to Michigan, was sent to Jackson again, and on his deathbed gave Gray the chart.
  - **Gray's search:** sand drift and wind had changed the trees so the spot was "not recognizable". He dug in several places but never deep for fear of cave-ins and gave up; "nothing was ever found, though up to his death Mr. Gray firmly believed that the story of the hidden treasure was true."
  - **Baty:** "Last year [1899] a man named **Baty, a mine owner and prospector**, passed through the city and heard the story from an old settler." His instrument "gave him a range directly south"; testing the whole range, "the attraction being so strong at one place, **the summit of the Five Mile hill**, that he commenced to dig." He gave up and left, then returned "a few days ago" with a partner, **Robinson**, also a locater. They hired diggers at $1 per foot and **have a hole 20 feet deep**.
  - **Site:** "After a delightful tramp through the woods **along the river ridge** and a climb through the soft sand one comes suddenly out on **the white summit of the Five Mile hill**", with Lake Michigan stretching to the south, east [sic] and west, and green hills to the north. "Just back of the summit, yet far above the highest tree top, is the spot where the fortune hunters are at work." The buckets bring up old willow branches and stumps; Baty takes this as proof of drifted sand and says he will go 60 ft. **At about 20 ft, part of a page of an old Grand Rapids Eagle of 1879 was found.** The shaft is shored with dovetailed boards; a windlass and cable are used.
  - Outcome not given in this article (PC a's Oct 1899 outcome stands for the first trip).

## 18. Newton 1868 county safe robbery: *The History of Jasper County, Iowa* (Western Hist. Co., 1878; HathiTrust wu.89064482938), "County Safe Robbery", pp.388-403 (seq 380-395)
- **Night of 25 Feb 1868:** the county safe (temporary deposits of the County Treasurer) at Newton was blown open with powder (fuse and spermaceti candle left behind) and **"robbed of about $3,500 in current funds."**
- **Five men** got off the westbound CRI&P train at Kellogg (the station E of Newton) on the night of the robbery. Five men were also seen getting on a train early next morning at Mitchellville (W of Newton). They walked into the country and hired farmer **William Reed** to drive them N to **Nevada** (C&NW Ry).
- In Newton, the lumber office of **Hough & Atwater** had been broken into the same night. **"Careful examination of the yard adjoining the office" turned up the county safe's money box (empty) and a coffin-shaped box of burglar's tools.** A blacksmith shop on the W side was also used.
- A Pinkerton detective (sent for by Eyerly, Winslow & Wilson through a Newton banker) followed them to Chicago, where police arrested them. **Charles Udell** and **Abraham Rose** pleaded guilty on 25 Nov 1868: Udell got 10 years, Rose 7. **"The $954 taken from them at the time of their arrest"** was applied to the costs. Udell was pardoned on 5 Jan 1878 (dying of consumption). Jail letters (offering $2,000 bribes for escape) were passed to Pinkerton and helped break up the **Reno gang** of Indiana.
- **Money gap: about $3,500 taken, $954 recovered; about $2,550 unaccounted for** (presumably split with the three other men). **The book says nothing about anything hidden or buried**; the only "yard" find is the empty money box beside Hough & Atwater's lumber office in Newton.
- Place: Newton town (the courthouse square and an adjoining lumber yard). No pin beyond the town; no cache claim.
