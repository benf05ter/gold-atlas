## 5. Wallaces' Farmer 1927 buried-treasure contest (HathiTrust full view: v.52 no.1-26 = rul.39030035793258; no.27-52 = rul.39030035793308)
- **The contest:** announced with Charlton Laird's article "When the Middle-West Was Young: Buried Treasure Stories Mark the Stamping Grounds of Old-Time Bandits", **29 Apr 1927, p.3 (cont. p.10)**: $5 for the best letter and $1 for each other one used. The closing date was printed as 7 May. **27 May 1927, p.21** ("Buried Treasure Yarns") corrects it to **7 June**.
- **No reader letters were printed as such** in either half of v.52. Searches for "treasure" and "buried treasure" find only Laird's three articles, a boys' and girls' serial and fiction. The entries went into Laird's thesis instead (Akins/Landreth, Brood/Gruver, Bergman/Marble: books/laird-1927-iowa-legends.md). So there is **no separate list of entrants** to extract here.
- **Laird's three articles: in-region items not already in research/:**
  - 29 Apr 1927:
    - **Dubuque, Kelly's cabin** (Kelly's lead mine): about spring 1868 a section-hand boy, "Freckles" Maloney, found **$3,300** in gold eagles in molasses and tea cans in the dead miner Kelly's cabin. FOUND; "more" rumoured about Dubuque and its docks.
    - **Cedar Rapids islands**: Osgood Sheppard's 1839 log inn (later the YMCA site) and the horse-thief / counterfeit band's island fort. Stolen money is said to be buried on the Cedar River islands above Cedar Rapids, some dredged away or drowned when the dam was raised.
    - **Horse Thief Cave N of Anamosa** (Jones Co) and **Dead Man's Gulch, Fayette Co**: named only, with no detail.
    - **Steamboat Rock caves** (Hardin Co): the Bunker band and the Rainsbargers; booty is said to remain in the soil.
    - **Grundy Co** county funds stolen (Herbert Quick's account); **Kossuth, Bremer and Linn** county robberies.
    - **Palmyra, WI** (Mar 1927): the hermit Blackmore brothers (Samuel, Robert, Albert) on **Miracle Creek**. Already in research/ (plats/blackmore-palmyra.md).
    - The rest is Laird's McGregor, Bellevue and southern Iowa material.
  - 10 Jun 1927, p.3 (cont. p.8):
    - Council Bluffs 1879 stump gold (Muir/Samuels 1854) and the John Wallace Davis Co $3,000 (1864): both out of the northern region.
    - The Humboldt Johnson whisky, already logged.
  - **30 Dec 1927, p.3 (cont. p.10), "Treasure Plow Deep, and Deeper":**
    - **County tally of legend sums.** Northern counties: Allamakee 3 ($135,000), Carroll 1 ($2,000), Cass 1 ($5,000), Clayton 3 ($150,000), Dickinson 1 ($1,500), Dubuque 2 ($40,000), **Emmet 1 ($25,000)**, **Hamilton 1 ($25,000)**, **Hardin 2 ($2,000)**, Humboldt 1 ($5,000), Jackson 3 ($140,000), Johnson 3 ($10,000), **Kossuth 2 ($1,000)**, Linn 2 ($5,000), Marshall 1 ($50,000), Muscatine 5 ($50,000), Polk 5 ($30,000), Scott 1 ($200). (The Emmet and Hamilton figures give the Gruver and Boone-mouth sums Laird's thesis omitted.)
    - **Valley Junction (West Des Moines), Polk Co:** Amos Clegg's octagonal concrete house on the **White Pole road**, N of Valley Junction "where it swings up a hill". It was built about 10 years before the Civil War (Fowler plan). Clegg buried the family fortune during an Indian scare and later followed the Mormons west, losing his memory. "Folks look out along the ridge to the north"; a clairvoyant said "south of an unpainted building". Not found.
    - **Des Moines:** Mrs. W. P. Dyer's house, where treasure hunters claim a chest of deeds and money is buried in the cellar.
    - **Iowa City:** a fortune "said to be founded on a pot of gold"; a citizen searching his house walls.
    - The rest is south of the line (Eddyville/Gunton $30,000, Thurman $45,000, Lockridge $75,000, Andrew Musquaki $100,000 (already in research), Bedford $90,000, Fire Island/Muscatine, Rising Sun Bidwell).
- **Not MN/WI entries:** none printed.

## 1-4. HathiTrust full-text "buried his money" / "buried his gold" / "buried treasure" / "hidden treasure" (title contains WI, MN or MI)
- **"buried his money", title Wisconsin: 1 hit.** *History of Green County, Wisconsin* (1884; loc.ark:/13960/t8w95gk1z, p.1050): a pioneer (bachelor until 1851) who, on his first night, cut the turf where his residence later stood, buried his money and hung his camp kettle over the spot. - **"buried his money", title Minnesota: 4 hits, all *Northwestern Reporter* v.134 (1912), p.533 (an Iowa case).** Appeal from District Court, **Story County, Iowa** (Judge C. G. Lee; opinion by Weaver, J.). **Fred Dannenbrink** died intestate on **22 Oct 1904**. His granddaughter (sole heir; later his administratrix) had him declared incompetent in Oct 1902 (guardian **John Banzhof**, discharged three months later). He had sold off about **135 acres** in small tracts (2-40 acres) in one neighbourhood and most of his personal property. Being angry at her, "he would bury, or had in fact buried, his money where she would get none of it". There was also an illegitimate son from Germany, **Fred Bierman** (or Dannenbrink). **NEW** (no Dannenbrink in research/). It is a Story Co IA lead: an estate of "considerable magnitude", a declared burial threat, and the death in 1904. Next steps: 1900 census, Story Co, for Fred Dannenbrink, then the Story Co plat (1896/1902) for the tracts.
- **"buried his money", title Michigan: 2 hits.** *Michigan Farmer* 29 Nov 1930 p.483 is children's fiction ("A Hermit's Thanksgiving"); *The Standard* v.54 is not relevant.
- **"buried his gold", title Michigan: 9 items, chiefly *Michigan [Pioneer &] Historical Collections* v.32 (1902; miua.0534625.0032.001), pp.261-263, "The Early History of Shiawassee County": "KNAGGS' BURIED GOLD".**
  - The story: when land speculators came in, trader **Whitmore Knaggs** "by foul means or otherwise" got some of their money and buried it "around **Knaggs' bridge**" on the Shiawassee River.
  - About 20 years later a man named **Hadd** dug in many places about the bridge. Just before the Civil War, strangers dug along the river at night. Storekeeper **Thomas B. Allen** at the present bridge was asked for "a certain spring, or a tree, or of the rock in the river". **E. O. Byam** was shown a map marking "the rock, the tree, and the spring" and showed the men the spring.
  - The late **Hon. N. G. Phillips** owned the land along the river where the trading post stood and disbelieved the story. Judge Josiah Turner saw Knaggs at his log house at the bridge bank in Aug 1840. Whitmore was buried at Fremont, and his son John at Newburg.
  - Source written for the volume by **E. L. Lyman**.
  - Knaggs is already a 3-source pin (placement-gaps-list #18). **This MPHC v.32 account may be one of those sources (dedupe check),** but the Phillips ownership of the post site and the spring/tree/rock map are details worth adding to the card.

## 12. GB Detroit 1873-77, "Grand Haven" + light house + gold / buried treasure
- GB has the **Detroit Advertiser and Tribune** and the **Detroit News**, but **not the Free Press**. "Grand Haven" light house gold gives 2,112 hits (too broad). "Grand Haven" buried treasure gives 35. The top hit is not Grand Haven but **a NEW lead (Calhoun Co MI):**
- **Detroit Tribune, Mon 14 Sep 1874, p.3, "Lost Treasure: The Owner in Hot Pursuit, a Good Prospect of Finding It"** (Battle Creek, 11 Sep 1874; reprinted in the weekly, 17 Sep 1874 p.3).
  - About 30 years earlier (about 1844) **John McArdill**, a jewellery pedlar from London, Canada, bought land in **Emmett Twp, about 2 mi from Battle Creek**. A few years later he was **robbed of $4,000-5,000 in gold and silver coin and jewellery** and kept quiet about it.
  - **An anonymous letter dated 12 Sep 1873**, posted at a Branch Co post office and written by an old and infirm foreigner, said the writer had worked for McArdill clearing his farm. Knowing of the treasure, he and a fellow workman robbed him, and "**secreted the treasure in a dry well** which they had recently discovered", in the **"Newbre" neighbourhood, on the S side of the Kalamazoo River**. The well was about **40 ft deep**; they threw down earth, stones and **several oak stumps**. The other man died, and the writer left and never came back.
  - The letter gave directions: the well is "so many paces less than half a mile north of the gravel road and about eight paces from the north and south road". McArdill consulted S. V. R. Johnson, Esq. They tried to buy the lot from **N. A. Burton**, who owned it (he refused), and Burton was made a confidant. **The well was found exactly as described.**
  - About Sep 10 1874 the workmen reached the oak stumps, which were taken out "in almost as sound condition as when thrown in". At the time of writing "**the treasure, if any, has not been reached**".
  - Follow-up, Battle Creek, 12 Sep: a visit found a depth of **40 ft** reached with no result, and McArdill decided to dig 10 ft more. The old well had caved in before being filled up. The correspondent measured **80 paces to the road and 155 paces to the half-mile stake**, matching the letter. **Elias Newbre**, one of the oldest settlers, said the well was in good condition 20 years before, partly filled about 12 years before, and filled entirely since. Men worked armed.
  - **Outcome (GB "McArdill" MI Sep 1874 - Dec 1876: 9 hits; Coldwater Republican 19 Sep, True Northerner (Paw Paw) 18 Sep, Detroit Tribune 24 Sep and 1 Oct 1874, Detroit News 24 Sep, Jackson Citizen 3 Nov 1874 and Detroit 19 Oct 1876 not opened). Jackson Daily Citizen, Thu 29 Oct 1874, p.1:** "The excitement about the buried treasure in the old well in Emmet township, Calhoun county, like Banquo's ghost, will not down. The belief has become almost universal ... that **McArdill obtained the money on the last day** operations were carried on. A neighboring farmer ... said the men who were digging had not reached the bottom when he went to dinner, but upon his return they were **hastily filling up the excavation**, and claimed they had found nothing." So it was **probably recovered secretly** (local belief); officially "nothing found".
  - Place: Emmett Twp, Calhoun Co, S of the Kalamazoo River, on **N. A. Burton's lot** in the Newbre neighbourhood. A plat target is the 1873 Calhoun atlas (Burton, Newbre, McArdill). No McArdill or Newbre exists in research/.

## 15. GB Milwaukee 1900-30, "gold coins" razing: 1 hit, a NEW lead
- **Milwaukee Sentinel, Fri 28 May 1915, p.1, "Still More Gold, Wreckers Believe: Laborers Confident Walls of Sixth Street Building Contain Untold Wealth / Rumor of $19,000 Pot"** (with a photo: "The circle marks the crevice in which the pot of gold was found". The foreman "arrived in time to get $80 of the treasure" and turned it over to his employers).
  - **On Tuesday (25 May 1915)**, laborers of the **Milwaukee Wrecking Co.** razing the house at **142 Sixth Street** found "a small can, containing **$1,000 or more in gold coins**", in one of the walls of the basement.
  - The money is believed to have been secreted by **Jesse Scholl**, "wealthy Milwaukee pioneer, now dead". The company, the foreman and Scholl's relatives scoff at the idea of more gold, but the laborers believe the walls hold more.
  - **Rumour:** along with the pot was found "a diagram giving the exact location of the first find and telling where to find **another pot, containing $19,000**". It was said to be in the hands of one of the finders, who was "out celebrating"; it was not produced. Men poked about the stones and dirt for overlooked coins and surveyed every crevice of the remaining walls.
  - **FOUND $1,000+; the $19,000 second pot is a rumour (unfound).** Place: 142 Sixth St, Milwaukee (old numbering, pre-1930 renumbering, so the modern address needs converting). No Scholl or 142 Sixth St exists in research/. A follow-up GB search (Scholl 1915) is worth one try.
