### Michigan (1941)
- **NEW: Walker Tavern, Cambridge Junction (Irish Hills; US-12 / M-50 crossroads), Tour 1, about p.392.** A tavern of 1833 at the Chicago Turnpike / Monroe Turnpike crossing. In its "murder room" a "gold-laden speculator and cattleman bound for the West" was found dead beneath the shattered window, "murdered and robbed of his money belt"; the crime was never solved. "**Some months afterward, the ground beneath one of the giant oaks in the inn [yard] showed traces of digging, presumably done by someone who assumed the money belt had been buried there.**" Nine Potawatomi graves lie under the large oak in the inn yard (the oak was cut level for an 1840 Whig speakers' platform). The tavern is now a state historic site. Not in research/ (dedupe: no Walker Tavern).
- **NEW: Devil's Hole, S of Van Buren State Park (S of South Haven), Tour about 17/18, p.530:** "a deep ravine in the dunes, choked with vegetation, which is believed to have been used years ago as **a cache for horse thieves**." A horse-thief hideout, with no treasure sum. Not in research/ (the Walworth "Devil's Hole" is a different, WI place).
- **De Tour / Espanore Island (St Marys River), Tour 17, p.553:** legend of pirates on the nearby islands; Espanore "supposed to have been named for a Spanish pirate who, fleeing American justice, made it his hide-out". No treasure claim; minor.
- **Great Lakes sunken treasure (General, p.122):** "Fortunes in gold coin, rich cargoes of copper…" (the Clarion, the Lexington of Algonac 1846, the New Brunswick 1859); shipwreck lore, out of scope for land pins.
- Tekonsha: Chief Tekonquasha's body is "believed buried somewhere in the present village" (no treasure).

### Iowa (1938)
- **Kelly's Bluff, Dubuque (City Tour, point 8): "directly behind St Raphael's Cathedral, S. end of Cardiff St., edge of the bluff."** Named for Thomas Kelly, "a parsimonious and eccentric miner and recluse" who came in 1832 to work the lead mine on the hill. "When he died on May 15, 1859, he left no chart to the whereabouts of his fortune, which he had **buried in the ground. Sums of $10,000, $1,200, $1,800, and $500 have been found over a period of 70 years at various points on his property.**" (This gives four find amounts. The $500 matches the 1914 reservoir find, and the location matches the GNIS Kelly's Bluff, S end of Cardiff St, behind the cathedral.)
- Horse Thief Cave (Wapsipinicon State Scenic Preserve, near Anamosa): only Indian burials found around its walls; no treasure.
- The Bellevue War (1837-40 outlaw gang) and New Buda / Pomutz's "strongbox" (worthless papers) are not treasure.

## 15. Grand Haven Sanborns (LoC 1912 and 1920 title sheets, "Water Facilities")
- **June 1912:** direct-pressure water works from 54 driven wells (built 1890). "**NOTE: $60,000 gravity system to be installed in summer of 1912. Plans call for reservoir at an elevation of 200 ft. above lake water level.**"
- **July 1920:** "Gravity and direct pressure system … **Two concrete reservoirs, cap'y ½ million galls each, constructed in 1912, located 200 ft. above water works.**"
- So the city's twin reservoirs were built in **1912** at about 200 ft above the lake, which can only be on the high dune: **Five Mile Hill** (or Dewey Hill). The key maps show no reservoir symbol on the sheets I read (they cover only built-up blocks). This dates the "tanks on the dune" to 1912, **12 years after Baty's 1900 summit shaft** and 10 years before the 1922 Ford sand sale. Exact siting: Ground check (the round concrete reservoirs should show on the 1938 ortho).

- **1909 Sanborn** (item sanborn02633_004; 73 sheets). The index lists "St. Raphael's Cath. Cathedral, &c." on **sheet 61**.
