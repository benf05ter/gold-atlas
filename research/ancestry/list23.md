### Michigan (1941)
- **NEW: Walker Tavern, Cambridge Junction (Irish Hills; US-12 / M-50 crossroads), Tour 1, about p.392.** A tavern of 1833 at the Chicago Turnpike / Monroe Turnpike crossing. In its "murder room" a "gold-laden speculator and cattleman bound for the West" was found dead beneath the shattered window, "murdered and robbed of his money belt"; the crime was never solved. "**Some months afterward, the ground beneath one of the giant oaks in the inn [yard] showed traces of digging, presumably done by someone who assumed the money belt had been buried there.**" Nine Potawatomi graves lie under the large oak in the inn yard (the oak was cut level for an 1840 Whig speakers' platform). The tavern is now a state historic site. Not in research/ (dedupe: no Walker Tavern).
- **NEW: Devil's Hole, S of Van Buren State Park (S of South Haven), Tour about 17/18, p.530:** "a deep ravine in the dunes, choked with vegetation, which is believed to have been used years ago as **a cache for horse thieves**." A horse-thief hideout, with no treasure sum. Not in research/ (the Walworth "Devil's Hole" is a different, WI place).
- **De Tour / Espanore Island (St Marys River), Tour 17, p.553:** legend of pirates on the nearby islands; Espanore "supposed to have been named for a Spanish pirate who, fleeing American justice, made it his hide-out". No treasure claim; minor.
- **Great Lakes sunken treasure (General, p.122):** "Fortunes in gold coin, rich cargoes of copper…" (the Clarion, the Lexington of Algonac 1846, the New Brunswick 1859); shipwreck lore, out of scope for land pins.
- Tekonsha: Chief Tekonquasha's body is "believed buried somewhere in the present village" (no treasure).

### Iowa (1938)
- **Kelly's Bluff, Dubuque (City Tour, point 8): "directly behind St Raphael's Cathedral, S. end of Cardiff St., edge of the bluff."** Named for Thomas Kelly, "a parsimonious and eccentric miner and recluse" who came in 1832 to work the lead mine on the hill. "When he died on May 15, 1859, he left no chart to the whereabouts of his fortune, which he had **buried in the ground. Sums of $10,000, $1,200, $1,800, and $500 have been found over a period of 70 years at various points on his property.**" (This gives four find amounts. The $500 matches the 1914 reservoir find, and the location matches the GNIS Kelly's Bluff, S end of Cardiff St, behind the cathedral.)
- Horse Thief Cave (Wapsipinicon State Scenic Preserve, near Anamosa): only Indian burials found around its walls; no treasure.
- The Bellevue War (1837-40 outlaw gang) and New Buda / Pomutz's "strongbox" (worthless papers) are not treasure.

## 5. Michigan History Magazine (HathiTrust full text, v.1-14, 1917-30; "buried treasure / gold / money", "hidden treasure")
- **NEW: Port Sheldon, Ottawa Co (Pigeon Lake / Lake Michigan, between Holland and Grand Haven).** Ralph Chester Meima, "A Forgotten City", *Michigan History* v.5 (1921), pp.408-409 (HathiTrust mdp.39015071220209). The 1830s Port Sheldon boom town of the Ottawa House hotel: "Another tale connected with Port Sheldon is the **hidden treasure legend**. It is said that one of the capitalists connected with the project, when he perceived that the undertaking must fail, **buried what money he had** in order to prevent it from falling into the hands of the receivers. The shifting sands covered the spot, and though later he searched for many a day the treasure still eluded him, the sands burying it ever deeper." Also: Forrest B. Knight, a backer, killed himself; the clerk Abraham Pike stayed on as custodian; "all that remains are a few decayed maples … that graced the front of the **Ottawa House**"; the lumber went into the big barn on the **Vander Meiten place, E of the Grand Rapids Boy Scout camp**. **Not in research/.** A place pin is possible from the Port Sheldon townsite (Port Sheldon Twp, at the Pigeon River / Pigeon Lake outlet).

## 14. Dubuque Sanborns (LoC; IIIF images viewed in the browser)
- **1909 Sanborn** (item sanborn02633_004; 73 sheets). The index lists "St. Raphael's Cath. Cathedral, &c." on **sheet 61**. Sheet 61 shows the cathedral block (Bluff, Emmett, St Marys, W 3rd), "St Raphael's Bdg", Fenelon Pl, Raymond Pl, the **4th St incline (elevator)** running up from Raymond Pl, and the "foot of steep hill" line. **No reservoir or water tank is drawn.** As expected, the 1909 sheet predates a 1914 reservoir.
- **1950 update** (sanborn02633_005, sheets 61 and 47): sheet 61 has the same layout (now with Clarke College), with **no reservoir**. **Sheet 47** (W of Fenelon Pl: Prospect, Austin/Raymond Pl, Cooper Pl, Gilmore, Summit, W 5th) shows, N of Prospect St, **block 9 lots 33-36 left vacant inside a red-lined boundary**, with a **6-in. water pipe** along Prospect St and "**impassable steep hill**" at the W end. That is a city-property or water-line notation, but **no reservoir symbol**.
- **Result:** the 1909/1950 Sanborns do **not show a reservoir** on Kelly's Bluff or the adjoining upland sheets 61/47. The 42.4944,-90.6726 round structure is probably outside the Sanborn coverage (sheets cover only built-up blocks), or it is a later, unrelated tank. **Not confirmed.**
- **Key location fix (WPA Iowa 1938):** Kelly's Bluff = "directly behind St Raphael's Cathedral, S. end of Cardiff St., edge of the bluff". On the Sanborn, the cathedral block is Bluff / Emmett / St Marys / W 3rd, and the bluff edge rises W of it toward Fenelon Pl. Finds totalling $10,000 + $1,200 + $1,800 + $500 were made "at various points on his property".
