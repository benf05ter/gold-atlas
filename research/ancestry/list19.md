## 4. FAY ravine, Nicollet Co MN (outlaw dugout found Dec 1890)
- **1899 Nicollet atlas, "Plat of Lake Prairie, Township 111 North, Ranges 26 & 27 West"** (TOC pp.6-7; viewer p.5 = W part, **viewer p.6, image 31617_072136-00514 = E part, T111N R26W, with the Minnesota River**):

  - This matches the 1900 census Bernard Fay (b. 1828 Ireland), Lake Prairie Twp.
- BLM (MN05 T111N R26W): sec 8 = 44.4277-44.4422 N, 93.9904-93.9701 W; sec 7 = 93.9903-94.0091 W.
- **GB #15 FOUND: St Paul Daily Globe, Sat 20 Dec 1890, p.2** (Le Sueur, 19 Dec), "…Raid of 1876 / An Impregnable Subterranean House Among Nicollet County Hills / One of the Men Wounded at Northfield Dies in the Grewsome Cave / Remarkable Discovery of a Party of Rabbit-Hunting Young Farmers":
  - The find was in **Lake Prairie township, Nicollet county**: "the subterranean rendezvous of the Younger and James boys … before and after their raid upon the Northfield bank in 1876".
  - The young farmers, out rabbit shooting, were "**traversing what is known as the Barney Fay ravine from its foot or base toward its head**" when one fell down an embankment "into the bed of a creek". Scrambling back up the bank, he found **an old weather-beaten door held by leather straps**.
  - Inside: a dug-out anteroom about 8 ft square, then a **blind log door** to a room about **20 × 16 ft** with bunks for about 30 men, benches, a No. 9 cook stove, a flour box, decks of cards, old newspapers and a copy of "Kit Carson". In the book was a letter dated **"Elysian, Minn., April 8"**, "Friend Jim", signed "Frank" (to James Younger, from Frank James), about horse stealing, a vigilance committee at Le Sueur and a suspect "Brown" (a Pinkerton?). It mentions meeting at the **"Half-Way"**.
  - Behind the cupboard box, **a second cave room stabled about 20 horses** at 18 hitching posts. On a post hung an old vest with 75 cents in old 25-cent paper money and an envelope addressed **"J. S. Rivers"**. A **skull and bones** lay under a pile of corn stalks (theories: a murdered detective, or a wounded Northfield raider). The place is described as "impregnable as Gibraltar" (the article continues beyond the part read).
  - **No treasure is described in the part read**; it is an outlaw-hideout story (probably embellished).
- **"Barney Fay ravine" = Bernard Fay's land**, so the ravine runs through/below the **Bernard Fay 80, W sec 8, T111N R26W**, down to a creek. The 1899 plate shows a creek through the W part of sec 8 by the platted cluster. **Pin upgraded: 44.432, -93.988, r300, grade C+** (the ravine on the Fay tract; the exact cave spot is not given).

## 22. SCHOLL, Milwaukee 1915 (GB "Scholl gold", Milwaukee, 20 May - 31 Dec 1915: 84 hits, mostly other Scholls)
- **Milwaukee Sentinel, Sat 29 May 1915, p.4 (follow-up):**
  - **Law:** under Wis. statutes ch.71, ss.1614-1618, the finder must notify the town clerk and advertise; if the owner does not appear within a year, the find is split between the town treasurer and the finder. **District Attorney Zabel** said the "owner of the property" is the rightful claimant and the heirs of the man who hid it have no legal right; there was no prosecution of those "said to have the gold".
  - **"Company 'Claims' Gold":** the Milwaukee Wrecking Co. says it owns the gold, since its contract with the property holders on Sixth Street bought the entire building, "including the brick wall in which the gold was found". Officials believe the money "is probably all spent".
  - Rain on Friday halted wrecking, but "considerable prospecting was conducted in the old building and in **two places adjoining**, which were said also to have been built by **Jesse Scholl**, the supposed hider of the gold".
  - "**The brick fireplace in the building at 146 Sixth street** had been probed for treasure; its steel cover had been removed, some bricks pried out", with tool marks showing a search for hollow places.
  - "Care was taken, however, that no outsider should get any of **the $19,000** that, according to a diagram said to be in the hands of one of the laborers, is secreted about the premises. **Guards were on duty all day.**"
- So the Scholl-built row is **142-146 Sixth St** (old numbering). The 1st find is about $1,000+ (recovered, disputed); the $19,000 is rumoured and unfound. **N/S Sixth and the modern address still need the 1914-15 city directory** (Ancestry 2469; paused).
- Other hits (Leader 26 May p.9; Sentinel 19 Jun p.9) are other Scholls (Marie C. Scholl's wedding). Not yet opened: Sentinel 26 May (likely the first report), 22 Jun, 5 Aug, 28 Nov, 19 Dec; Leader 28 May, 16 Jun, 9 Nov.
