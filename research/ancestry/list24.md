## 18. "Leggatt's Hill" vs "Five Mile Hill", Grand Haven (1912 Sanborn and 1912 Ottawa plat)
- **(a) 1912 Sanborn** (LoC sanborn04021_005, June 1912, 24 sheets):
  - **Index:** **"Leggat" is a STREET**, nos. 400-447, on **sheet 15**. **Lake Ave.** (400-513) is on the same sheet 15. Under specials, "City Water Works & Electric Light Plant" is on sheet 17.
  - **Sheet 15:** Leggat St and Lake Ave run SW from Howard / Main St at the **SW edge of the city** (S 2nd / S 3rd St). **W of Leggat St (block 13) the map is labelled "VACANT SAND HILL"**, with a dotted contour running through the back lots; near Butler St / S 3rd, the E margin has a "Steep Sand Hill" label. The inset on the same sheet is **Highland Park Station, "1¾ miles S.W. of Grand Haven P.O."**, on the lake beach.
  - **Sheet 17:** the water works (54 driven wells, Walker and Worthington pumps, a 20-ft well) is on the **Grand River flats** at Madison Ave / N. Ferry, not on the hill. The hill reservoirs were not yet drawn (the June 1912 title note says they were to be installed that summer; see list23 #15).
  - **No "Leggatt's Hill" or "Five Mile Hill" label anywhere** on the index or on sheets 15/17.
- **(b) 1912 Ottawa plat, Grand Haven Twp (viewer p.29, image 31617_072124-00302), NW sec 29 and adjoining:** S. L. Munroe 46.45, the **"City of Grand Haven"** strip (on the shore just S of the city line), J. Mulligan, S. Walker, Mrs. E. W. Duncan 65, Lake Forest Cem., Fannie Ahrens 60 and 40, E. B. / W. H. Moore; Munroe Park / Lake View Add. / Highland Park on the dune. **No Leggatt tract in the township part of sec 29.** No water-works or tank parcel is marked.
- **Reading:** "Leggatt's Hill, just off Lake Ave" (1912 Tribune) is the **vacant sand hill directly W of Leggat St, at the SW corner of the city**. That is the **N (city) end of the dune ridge** that runs SW past Lake Forest Cemetery to Highland Park, the ridge called Five Mile Hill. The city-owned strip in NW sec 29 sits on the same ridge just S. So the two names very likely describe the same dune: "Leggatt's Hill" for its city end by Leggat St, and "Five Mile Hill" for the whole ridge. **No single source says so outright** (inference from street geometry).
- **For the pin:** the 1912 tanks belong at the dune crest W of Leggat St / Lake Ave (SW Grand Haven, near the cemetery), at about 200 ft above the water works. That is the harbour (N) end of the ridge that Ford's sand company mined from 1922 (list21 #12). Ground check: the 1938 aerial should show two round tanks there.

## 11. Dubuque reservoir on Kelly's Bluff (1914-15)
- **NEW, key source: Dubuque Water Dept., *Annual Report* 1910/11-1914/15** (HathiTrust full view, **uiug.30112105674953**). These are the trustees' reports; the 1914/15 report is at seq 37-38.
  - Seq 37 (betterment account): **"Reservoir - Real estate (Out Lot No. 732) 10,000.00."**
  - Seq 38: in 1913-14, "$10,656.20 on account new reservoir, in which amount is included **$10,000.00 paid for the reservoir site on Kelly's Bluff**". The original plan, a reservoir on the hill in **Eagle Point Park** by the main pumping station, was **abandoned on the engineers' advice**. "A site for the reservoir was secured on **Kelly's Bluff, namely Out Lot No. 732**, situated reasonably close to the lower end of the 20-inch main feeder of the system and the high value district of the City … favorably located geographically and as to elevation." The **capacity was to be 7½ million gallons**; plans were being prepared and a contract was expected **in about 60 days**.
  - So the site was **bought in 1914** and the basin dug from **1915**. The "$500 found by workmen digging a reservoir on the old claim, 1914" (Des Moines Register 1936, newspapers-com/list20 #6) fits this job; the find was probably in 1915.
- **For the pin:** the reservoir is on **Dubuque Out Lot 732** on Kelly's Bluff. Next step: find Out Lot 732 on a Dubuque plat (1906 or 1919 Dubuque atlases, or a county GIS "OUT LOT 732" legal-description search). A 7.5-million-gallon basin is large, so the round or rectangular structure near 42.494,-90.673 (list23 #14) is a likely match. **Grade can rise once Out Lot 732 is placed.**
- **WPA *Dubuque County History* (1942, uiug.30112049819987), pp.17-18:** Tom Kelly (sandy-haired Irish miner) owned the entire bluff, bought for about $300. He shipped lead by barge to St Louis, and in 1847-48 to New Orleans and New York, where he shot a man, was declared insane and escaped home. **He died in 1867** (the 1938 state guide says 1859). **"One story is that Tom Kelly had an iron chest made in Galena, brought it to his cabin on Kelly's Bluff, and put most of his money in it. This chest was never seen after his death, but smaller caches, ranging from a few dollars to $10,000, were unearthed."** The large chest is already in research (the DM Register 1936 "blacksmith-made" chest); the **Galena** origin is new colour. The same book (p.64) covers the waterworks history (1864 drainage tunnel, 1888 Delhi St standpipe, city purchase 1900) but not the 1914 reservoir.
- Council proceedings 1914-15: not on HathiTrust or archive.org.

## 7. Page 1882, *History of Ottawa County* (HathiTrust miun.bad1034.0001.001), "The Port Sheldon Bubble, 1836-38", pp.35-36
- The New York and Philadelphia Port Sheldon Company first wanted Grand Haven; when refused, it chose the **north side of Pigeon Lake** (Olive Twp) in fall 1837. Saunders Coates was general superintendent; A. H. Judson and E. P. Deacon were superintendents; G. M. Barker surveyor; **Abraham Pike clerk**.
- The plat had 142 blocks of 24 lots. Roads were built to Grandville and Grand Haven ($5-10,000), and a **lighthouse** at the company's own expense.
- **"A splendid hotel at an expense of thirty to forty thousand dollars … 60x120 feet"** (the Ottawa House), an office costing $10,000, a store, "the best mill in the West" ($20,000) and 15 small dwellings. Pike stayed as sole occupant and agent for years, trying to sell the hotel and 30 lots.
- **No buried-money or treasure passage in Page 1882.** The legend's earliest text found is still Meima 1921 (GR Press, Dec 1921 series; *Michigan History* v.5).

## 15. GB Michigan 1880-1930, "Walker tavern" + murdered: 14 hits (all 1919-27)
- **Earliest found: Jackson Citizen Patriot, Sun 26 Oct 1919, p.28, "Cambridge Junction, Village Just Over the Jackson County Line, Links Present With Bygone Days Hamlet".**
  - **"The Murder Room"** is on the second floor, north of the Cooper rooms. It got its name because "one dark night a guest **disappeared, never being heard from again**. The fact that this guest had **considerable money** with him led to the theory that someone had lured him away and murdered him for his money, or else murdered him in the room and carried the body away."
  - The tavern "also carries buried in its walls mysteries … It has its murder room and its suicide room."
  - **In 1853 Walker, "who had made a fortune in the hotel business", built the three-story brick building across the corner** (third-floor ballroom; closed since WWI). The DAR (Adrian) and the Adrian Automobile Club planned to buy and restore it.
  - **No digging under an oak and no money belt** in 1919 ("dug": 0 hits).
- **Kalamazoo Gazette, Sat 23 Sep 1922, p.6, "Inn of Stage Coach Days Is Reopened on Chicago Highway":** the tavern of 1832 was restored and reopened; "one **gold-laden traveler was murdered**. The stain of his blood is still on the floor of the so-called 'murder room'", shown on "personally conducted" tours.
- **So the legend grew over time:** a vanished guest with money (1919), then a "gold-laden traveler" murdered with a blood stain (1922 reopening publicity), then by 1941 (WPA) the cattleman found dead under a shattered window, his money belt gone, and digging under the inn-yard oak. The oak-digging detail is **not yet found before 1941**. The other 12 hits (Jackson News 1922/23, Adrian Telegram 1922/27, Detroit News 1922, Ann Arbor News 1927, Kalamazoo 1926) are not opened yet.
- Grade note: a 1922 tourist-inn story with no contemporary murder report, so the treasure angle (a buried money belt) is weak. Keep the pin at the historic site as a legend site.

## 16. GB Grand Rapids 1890-1930, "Wabasis" + gold: 8 hits (Wabasis is already a lead, leads.tsv row 111; these add detail)
