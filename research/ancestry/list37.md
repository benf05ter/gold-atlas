# List 37: county-history lead mining, round 2 (PC b, 2026-11-08)

**Method:** as list 34. archive.org full-text OCR (kept in scratch for grep only) for about 100 volume files across the 20 groups.
- **Phrases:** buried treasure, pot of gold, hidden money/treasure/gold, buried (his) money/gold, money was buried/hidden, counterfeiter(s), robbers' cave, lost mine, Spanish coin, hid his money, kettle of gold, treasure trove, dug for money, bogus (coin/camp/den/money/island), money/gold/coins was found.
- Page numbers come from the OCR running heads (±1). **All hits are secondary, so D unless a paper confirms.**
- **Skipped as known:** Bogus Island in Michigan (Leoni / Portage / Bawbeese, lists 19/25/26/28); the Hardin Co gold diggings; the Fraser / Pilot Mound Brown work (list25).
- **Noise dropped:** boilerplate (Mound Builder "buried treasures", Joseph Smith, Lewis & Clark); "counterfeit" money passed in a shop or court case; bank failures; the Delaware IA Dillon story (that was Dubuque's Miners' Bank, not a cache); the Boone IA Redmon money (dug up and recovered the same night).

## NEW LEADS (ranked)

1. **NEW BALTIMORE, Macomb Co MI: English officer's buried bullion.** *History of Macomb County* (Leeson 1882), p.~484-485 (historyofmacombc00lees).
   - A tradition with "many believers even now": an English Captain or Lieutenant who had gathered "a quantity of bullion", fleeing the Indians, "buried his treasure in the earth about a mile from the present site of New Baltimore". He was killed or died, "the secret of his treasure's hiding-place died with him". "Many searches have been made", and a ghost is said to guard it and move it.
   - **Context:** the passage sits under "Tremble Creek" (Capt. François Marsac's settlement about 1798, the creek just this side of New Baltimore on the Ridge road) and Swan Creek (4 mi beyond). **Pin: about 1 mi from New Baltimore toward Tremble Creek / Ridge Rd, village-level r1500, D.** This is the best treasure item in this round.
2. **BOGUS CORNERS, Lyon Twp, Oakland Co MI.** *History of Oakland County* (Durant 1877), p.215 (Lyon Twp chapter).
   - "Samuel Barton, of bogus coin fame … settled at what … has since been known as BOGUS CORNERS." In 1877 the Corners were owned by Walter Bowers, George W. Button and Jonathan Taylor. Gang: Henry Eddy (died of poison) and Peter Loomis, a blacksmith and the principal workman, later sent to the penitentiary.
   - **Placeable:** an 1872 Oakland plat (Ancestry 1127, image 31617_072123-00816 set) for Bowers / Button / Taylor at a Lyon Twp crossroads, near New Hudson. Not run this pass. D.
   - Same volume p.263: **Oakland Twp**, a man named **Murwin** kept a drug store that was "a resort for horse-thieves and counterfeiters". Neighbours bombarded it and he fled. Colour.
3. **OVID Twp, Clinton Co MI: Vansickle counterfeiters' den.** *History of Shiawassee and Clinton Counties* (1880), Ovid chapter.
   - "**William Vansickle** made a small clearing and put up a cabin on the **south half of section 31**" (summer 1838). Henry Leach of Sciota tipped off Detroit. A posse caught Vansickle, a woman, and Ward, Skiff and Gridley "manufacturing counterfeit Mexican dollars". Vansickle walked the bogus dollars to Detroit in a black valise.
   - **Pin: S½ sec 31, Ovid Twp (T7N R1W), r400, D.** It is section-placed.
   - Same volume, **Dallas Twp**: "a trifling bit of business done on **Stony Creek** in the matter of manufacturing spurious coin". Colour.
4. **MANISTEE: the "Old House" block-house of the counterfeiters.** *History of Manistee County* (Page 1882, in the Mason/Manistee volume mi-mason-1882-page).
   - Massachusetts men built a strong block-house up the Manistee River in **sec 36, T22N R14W** (before 1849; Indians stopped their dam). "At a very early day this old block-house was taken possession of by a gang of counterfeiters" who made bogus dollars there. It was standing "until a few years ago".
   - **Pin: sec 36 T22N R14W on the Manistee River, r400, D.** Section-placed.
5. **HARTLAND Twp, McHenry Co IL: counterfeiters' cave.** *History of McHenry County* (1885), p.697.
   - Early counterfeiters of silver coin: "**their lair was a sort of a cave in the timber, covered over with planks and sward**", with their shanty about a mile away in the timber. They left suddenly.
   - Hartland = T45N R6E (per the text). No section. D.
6. **ALGONQUIN, McHenry Co IL: Bogus Island.** Same volume, p.389. Counterfeiters frequenting Algonquin village "were finally tracked to **Bogus Island, about a mile further up the stream** [Fox River], where they were captured". This was Allan Pinkerton's first detective case.
   - **Note:** Kane 1878 p.230 tells Pinkerton's capture of "Old Craig" (taken to Geneva). Pinkerton's island is usually placed near Dundee, so it may be the same island. **Pin: Fox River about 1 mi upstream (N) of Algonquin, r800, D.**
7. **INLET GROVE (Lee Co IL): Bliss "Travelers' Home" log tavern.** *History of Ogle County* (1878), p.~303-355 ("Prairie Pirates").
   - Adolphus Bliss, Corydon Dewey and Charles West ("Bliss, Dewey, West & Co.") were the first settlers at **Inlet Grove** (about 1835-36). Bliss's "Log Tavern / Travelers' Home" was a "rendezvous for counterfeiters, or … a distributing point for their currency and coin". Repeated in Winnebago IL 1877 p.263.
   - No section. **Pin: Inlet Grove, Lee Co, village-level, D.** Not yet in leads.tsv (grep negative).
8. **AUSTIN MN: Loveland's coin moulds.** *History of Mower County* (1911), p.157.
   - Winfield Loveland, a blacksmith and fast-horse man, had log buildings on land "now occupied by Mrs. G. M. Cameron". After he left, "**moulds for making gold and silver coins were found secreted between the logs**" of his stable. Urban Austin; it needs a Cameron lot. D, colour.
9. **CEDAR RAPIDS / Otter Creek, Linn Co IA.** *History of Linn County* (1878), p.~341-347.
   - Stephen Snooks's cabin "on the place now owned by Richard Jackman", whose foundation is still visible (Otter Creek region), harboured "thieves and counterfeiters".
   - The Leverich and Shepard house gang at the Rapids.
   - Also Hiram Roberts (hanged 1857, confessed to over $100,000 spurious), who stayed at James W. Hanlin's, 4 mi NW of Tipton (Cedar Co).
   - Colour, D. Snooks/Jackman is placeable on a Linn plat.
10. **BOONE Co IA: counterfeiters' headquarters.** *History of Boone County* (Goldthwait 1914) v.1, p.277.
    - A band of horse thieves and counterfeiters "had headquarters among the hills along the river between **Centerville, in Yell Township**, and the center line of **Pilot Mound** Township". The text says "no one has been able to give the exact location". It is near the Fraser area of earlier lists. D.
11. **Smaller colour (no spot):**
    - **St. Joseph Co MI 1877 p.47:** a counterfeit gang on the S border 1850-57, crossing into Indiana; E. W. Pendleton of Sturgis.
    - **Washtenaw 1881:** "the den of the counterfeiters" near Godfrey's old trading post (Ypsilanti), later "turned into an ordinary sand hole".
    - **Macomb 1882 p.719:** A. B. Cooley "most expert counterfeiter of Utica", and Jennings the counterfeiter in a cedar swamp plot.
    - **Cedar IA 1878 p.532:** Gove, Conlogue and Goodell's whisky shop, W bank of the Cedar at Washington Ferry, 1838, "accused of being counterfeiters".
    - **La Crosse 1881 p.539:** four coin counterfeiters arrested in a house on the edge of the prairie, lower part of the city.
    - **Waukesha 1880 p.~700-701:** a mesmerist claimed stolen store money was sunk in a can in the lake; the lake was dragged with no result (village not checked).
    - **Benton IA 1878 p.336:** the Linn-line gang.
    - **Whiteside 1877 p.274:** counterfeiters till 1858, caught by Clendenin.
    - **Scott 1882 p.671:** an arrest in Meyers' saloon, Brady St.
    - **Morrison 1915:** "hidden treasure" = iron ore (noise).
