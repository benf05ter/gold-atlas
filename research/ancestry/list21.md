## 2. Jesse Scholl probate, Milwaukee Co (case no. 6470, probate 12 Jun 1886; Ancestry 9088, Milwaukee packets 6465-6492, 1886; inventory images 007706539_00342 to 00351, filed 6 Oct 1886, Inventories vol. 41 p.37)
- **He was a moneylender and investor.** Personal property is pages of **mortgage notes** (dozens of loans of $500-$1,700 at 7-8%), **bonds** (US 4% registered and coupon bonds; Chicago, Milwaukee & St Paul Ry consolidated 7% bonds), 2 shares of the Milwaukee Industrial Exposition Assn, certificates of deposit (Marshall & Ilsley), a note of J. A. Schoemaker, and household furniture and clothing ($250).
- **Section J, cash:** "The sum of $1066.44 [?] was found in currency which amount deceased was to turn over to A. S. Barndt [?] as trustee per order of Court, which amount has been paid over to said A. S. Barndt July 19/86." Then "Currency & coin" items (about $818.5x, $28.75, $20.00, $605.27), totalling **about $1,477.56**, plus a US Treasurer interest check $40 and a draft on bank $15. So **only about $1,500 in currency and coin was found in the house** and inventoried; **no gold hoard is listed**.
- **Recapitulation (image 00350):** total personal property about **$93,469.02**; real estate in Milwaukee about **$26,000** plus other real estate (Manitowoc lots; Faribault Co MN quarter-sections, secs 13-14 T103N R25W) about **$7,515**; **total appraised estate about $126,984**.
- **Real estate (image 00349):** the first parcel is **"the South fifty (50) feet of Lot four (4) in Block No. Sixty-seven (67) in the Fourth Ward of the City of Milwaukee"**, $11,000. The second is the S½ of Lot 13 in Block 74, Fourth Ward ($10,000), and the third is Lot 1 in Block 121, Second Ward ($5,000; "buildings much dilapidated"). **The Fourth Ward Block 67 / 74 lots = the Sixth St buildings** (the Fourth Ward was W of the river; Sixth St runs through it). The S 50 ft of Lot 4, Block 67, is a strong candidate for 142-146 Sixth St.
- **Reading:** an orderly estate of about $127,000, mostly paper. The 1915 can of about $1,000 in gold coins in the wall would have been missed by the 1886 appraisers (they recorded about $1,478 in currency/coin found). The probate neither proves nor rules out more hidden coin; it shows he kept cash in the house.

## 8. Knaggs in the Shiawassee histories
- HathiTrust: "Knaggs" appears in the 1880 *History of Shiawassee and Clinton Counties* (several copies), *Past and Present of Shiawassee County* (1906, miua.4763144.0001.001), *Shiawassee County Illustrated* and the St. Clair/Shiawassee sketches. The 1880 history has no "gold"/buried passage in the snippets.
- ***Past and Present* (1906), p.17:** a party of land speculators visited the post, and Knaggs "suddenly came into possession of a large amount of gold", whether by gambling or "some manner less fair". The report "that he had secured it … and **buried it** for safe keeping" brought "adventurers from distant states to dig for the buried treasure of **Shiawassee's Captain Kidd**". **Hadd** dug about 20 years later around Knaggs Bridge; **about 1860** strangers dug along the river by night. Since the younger Knaggs' death, strangers have asked for "a certain **spring** and a **tree**" and for "**a rock in the river known as 'Indian Rock'**"; a party also called on **E. O. Byam**. (This matches MPHC v.32. **New detail: the rock's name, "Indian Rock".**)

## 12. GB "Five Mile Hill" treasure, MI 1900-30: 2 hits
- GR Herald 23 Apr 1900 (already logged in list17).
- **NEW: Detroit Evening Times, Tue 22 Aug 1922, p.2, "By Ford: Detroiter to Start a Glass Manufacturing Plant in Grand Haven"** (Grand Haven, 22 Aug). **"Henry Ford has purchased the famous five-mile sand dune"** through the Grand Haven Sand Co.; the city got $1 per car, shipping **20 cars a day** to Detroit (foundry cores, perhaps windshields). "**Sale of the harbor end of the famous dune**…" Under "Story of Treasure": local tradition "located Five-Mile Hill as the hiding place of a fabled treasure … buried beneath the five miles of sand long years ago", whether "chests of gold of Capt. Kidd type"; "**veteran resorters certify the tale of at least one mysterious expedition which dug for several days. What they found … nobody knows.**"
- **Key for the pin:** from 1922 the **harbor (N) end of Five Mile Hill was mined away for Ford** (sand mining), so the 1900 dig spot at the summit may no longer exist as terrain. Flag this for Ground check.
