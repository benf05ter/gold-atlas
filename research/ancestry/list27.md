## 10. Ruggles Block, Bronson (Branch Co MI): PLACED (Sanborn 1897 / 1910 / 1916, LoC)
Sources: LoC Sanborn Bronson 1897 (sanborn03935_003, 2 sheets), 1910 (sanborn03935_004, 2 sheets), Sept 1916 (sanborn03935_005: the 1910 sheets reused, plus new sheets 1916-0001 to 0004). View-only IIIF crops.
- **1916, sheet 3 (03935_1916-0003), NE corner of the sheet:** a **"POST OFFICE"**, 2-storey brick, on the **S side of W. Chicago St, the 3rd building W of Matteson St**. The row from the SW corner of Matteson and Chicago runs west: Hardw. (lot 36, corner; harness shop behind), Meat & Gro., **POST OFFICE (lot 37)**, then Printing Office (no. 508). Behind are a garage, then the Town Hall / Fire Dept on Matteson.
- **1910, sheet 1 (03935_1910-0001), same spot:** the same 2-storey brick building, lot 37, is labelled **"Books"** (front "open 1st on iron posts"), between Meat and the vacant no. 508. **J. F. Ruggles was a rare-book dealer** (Kalamazoo Gazette 1915-03-09 p.2 and the Detroit News / Coldwater 1915 tellings, already logged). So the 1910 bookshop became the 1915-16 post office, which matches "the brothers sold the building (now the post office)". **This is the Ruggles Block.**
- **Cross-check:** in 1897 and 1910 the post office was elsewhere: a small Jewelry & P.O. building at the E end of the S-side block E of Matteson (lot B). By 1916 that lot is "Jewelry" only, so the post office moved between 1910 and 1916.
- The Sanborn shows 2 storeys outside. The legend's "6 floors inside" is not shown (Sanborn does not draw half-floors or attics).
- **Pin: 41.8721, -85.1950, r25, B** (3rd lot W of Matteson on the S side of W. Chicago St (US-12); about 30 m W and 25 m S of the Matteson/Chicago centre-line crossing at about 41.87235,-85.19468, measured on a USGS orthophoto). The orthophoto still shows buildings on this frontage. Whether the 1880s-1900s building survives needs a street-level look (Ground check). Look-only: an occupied downtown building.
- **Survival (Google Street View, view-only, imagery about 2023, from the pano at about 108 W Chicago St):** the S side of W. Chicago St W of Matteson is still a row of 2-storey brick fronts. From the Matteson corner going W: green-painted corner building (= 1916 Hardw.), red brick (= Meat & Gro.), **3rd: cream/white-brick 2-storey with 3 upper windows and a red awning (= the 1916 Post Office / 1910 "Books" = Ruggles Block)**, then tan brick (= Printing Office). **The building appears to stand.** The match is by count and width from the corner; there is no name plaque or date stone legible.

## 6. Ritter / Enes brewery, Henderson (Sibley Co MN): placed approximately (Sanborn 1894 / 1910, LoC)
- **LoC Sanborn Henderson June 1894** (sanborn04308_001, sheet 1 inset) and **Nov 1910** (sanborn04308_003, sheet 1 inset) both show **"Jno. (John) Enes Brewery, located 1/2 mile S. of P.O."** It is the only brewery on either set.
  - The building is a brick brewhouse (malt house, malt mill, kiln, ice houses, wash house and cooler; capacity 3,000 bbls in 1910), sited at the foot of a **"Vacant High Hill"** on its W side, with a **"Drive"** along the E front.

## 11. "Benton Lake" lumber village NE of Fremont (Newaygo Co MI): candidate found (1880 atlas)
- Dedupe: plats/benton-lake-newaygo.md already shows that today's Benton Lake (sec 20 T15N R13W, 43.6714,-85.8884, about 14 mi NNE of Fremont) is **"Blue L." on the 1900 atlas**, with Baker Lumber Co. land on its E side. That answers the "was it Blue Lake" question: yes, in 1900.
- **1880 *Atlas of Newaygo County*** (archive.org 3927793.0001.001.umich.edu). The plates are leaves 13-61, with Sheridan = leaf 53 and Dayton = leaf 55. **Sherman Twp, T13N R13W, leaf 43** is the township diagonally NE of Fremont (Sheridan Twp). On it (read by eye at scale 4):
  - **Robinson or Big Lake** (secs 10/3/11): on the SE shore, in S sec 10, is a **"St. Shingle Mill"** (steam shingle mill) with **Wetzel Bros. 68.64**. Pearson & Wilcox and Heald, Murphy & Crepin (lumbermen) hold the land around it.
  - **Worcester Sta.** (sec 15; today "Wooster") on the Chicago & West Michigan R.R., with a second "St. Shingle Mill" and a cluster of houses: a small rail and mill hamlet about 1 mi S of the lake.
  - **Alleyton** (sec 1, NE corner; Jas. Alley & Co.), a platted lumber village on the White River.
  - Other lakes: Crystal L. (sec 21), Peck L., Ford L., Long L. (with a **Dam** at its S outlet, sec 34). Most of the E half is owned by Ryerson, Hills & Getty (Muskegon lumbermen).
- **Reading:** the only "mill or lumber village on a lake" a few miles NE of Fremont in 1880 is the **Robinson / Big Lake shingle-mill site with Worcester station**. Today's Robinson Lake (OSM) is at **43.5318, -85.8545** (Jugville / Sherman Twp), about **6 mi NE of Fremont**, which fits "several miles NE" far better than Benton Lake (14 mi). No "Benton" name appears on the 1880 plate.
- **Suggested pin for the mill site:** SE shore of Robinson Lake, S sec 10 T13N R13W, about **43.527, -85.850, r300, C** (BLM: sec 10 T13N R13W = 43.5246-43.5390 N, -85.8612 to -85.8411 W; the mill symbol sits near the S line, a little E of centre). It's an alternative to the Benton Lake pin: a candidate, not a proven identification. Dayton Twp (E of Fremont) was not read in detail.
