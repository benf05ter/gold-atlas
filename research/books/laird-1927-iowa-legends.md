# Laird 1927, *Iowa Legends of Buried Treasure* (M.A. thesis, State University of Iowa): in-region chapters (PC b, 2026-11-02)

Source: HathiTrust full view **iau.31858060747148** (read in the text-only view, page at a time; not downloaded). Page numbers are Laird's.

## Boone Gold: Webster and Hamilton Counties, "Hints of an Indian Treasure" (pp.85-86)
- **Informant:** a letter from **Miss Ruby Akins, Stratford, Ia.**, to *Wallaces' Farmer*, 4 June 1927, "c/o Y. Landreth".
- **Story:** "dating back as far as **1849**, horse thieves ... hid their loot in an **Indian mound, at the mouth of the Boone River**". The mound "is covered with a thick cluster of trees", "**near the Lott monument**" (Henry Lott, first white settler, whose family was massacred). "This mound can be found on the **farm of Y. Landreth** who is a survivor of the 50's." "This treasure has been dug for but **not found**."
- **Place:** the mouth of the Boone River (into the Des Moines River), by the Lott massacre monument, which is SW of Stratford and NE of Boone, at the Webster/Hamilton/Boone county junction. Treasure is the loot from horse thieves; no amount given.
- Laird cross-refs: "Andrew Treasure" and Appendix fn.78.

## Livermore Whiskey: Humboldt (and Dickinson) Counties, "A Legend of Volatile Treasure" (pp.87-88)
- **Informant:** Jack Barnes of Des Moines, a student who once worked in Humboldt County (oral).
- **Story:** six hogsheads of whiskey, "and some money too", buried by three trader brothers named **Johnson**, fearing the Indians around the time of the **Spirit Lake massacre (1857)** (after Lott killed Sidominadotah). They "rolled all these hogsheads out back of their log cabin and buried them in the woods"; "the house stood right where the barn stands now". The Indians killed all three, and "nobody was ever able to find that whiskey".
- **Place:** "on the **William Cox farm, about a mile and a half east of Livermore**, in Humboldt County". There is a grove, and the spot was by the Sunday baseball field. The informant adds "**gold buried all along Bloody Run**" (small settler caches; the river shifts). **Not found.**
- Cross-ref: Appendix fn.8/75.

## Spirit Lake Treasure: Dickinson County (p.89)
- **Informant:** a letter from **Mrs. Minnie Kingman Bergman of Spirit Lake** (b. Spirit Lake 1861), via *The Des Moines Register* contest.
- **Story:** the **Marble family** (killed in the 1857 massacre, one daughter captive) "lived on the **west shore of Spirit Lake** on the farm that is now the **Birl Maish farm**"; when Mr. Marble found the Indians were coming he had **$1,500 in gold** buried "some place on that farm and it has **never been found**; the pioneers used to hunt for it". Laird's note: "Indians are said to have taken $1,000 from Marble's body" (Sharp, *History of the Spirit Lake Massacre*, pp.66-71, 82 ff.).
- **Place:** the Marble cabin site, W shore of Spirit Lake (the "Maish farm" in 1927); the informant lived about 2 mi away.

## Bellevue Bandit Gold: Jackson County, "A Pest House and the Root of All Evil" (pp.90-91)
- **Informant:** Mrs. Anna Silson of Bellevue (oral).
- **Story:** "the old pest house", a **stone house "southwest of town"** built by horse thieves when she was a girl; noises, shootings; vacant after the McDonnells moved out. "Up back of the house a ways is a **cave**"; below it a farmer plowing broke into an **underground room** (kettles, cooking things). Later the **Halfhill** party found **four skeletons** deep in the cave. **Theodore Winekopf** bought the house, and his daughter, digging in the cellar, found a big ring that a banker called a **counterfeiter's ring**. Money "buried there may be counterfeit". **Nothing else found.**
- **Place:** the stone "pest house" SW of Bellevue, with a hillside cave behind it.

## Gruver Gold: Emmet County, "A Horse Thieves' Treasure" (pp.91-93)
- **Informant:** a letter from **Wilbur Brood**, via *Wallaces' Farmer* (the story is set "in the time of my grandfather", though the letter says "1895", which looks like a typo for the 1850s-60s).
- **Story:** a band of horse thieves found an **island in a seldom-visited lake** in NW Iowa near Gruver, built an underwater **stone walk** to it, a barn and a **house with a stone cellar**, and raided for 3-5 years. The house burned and they moved SW, where all were killed except an old man, who came back and said the leader buried the treasure in the stone cellar, **8 ft east of the centre** (found by stretching cords between corners). After two weeks' search the stone road and cellar were dug out, but **nothing found**; it is suspected the old man took it.
- **Place:** an island in a lake near Gruver. The lake is not named in the letter; it is likely **Ingham Lake / High Lake / Swan Lake area** (Emmet Co lakes near Gruver), still to identify.

## Andrew Treasure: Jackson County, "Our Liberal Uncle and a Beleaguered Musquaki" (pp.51-53)
- **Informant:** **W. Herschelman**, a farmer aged about 55, living on the edge of **Andrew** (the old county seat) (oral).
- **Story:** about **$100,000** in government (annuity) money held by a Sac/Fox ("Musquaki") Indian who sold out to the whites. He fled from the Sioux and was caught and killed, but had already hidden the money "along the **Indian trail that used to run between [Andrew] and where the iron bridge is now, maybe along Brush Creek**". The informant thinks it is in a cave or hollow tree, not a mound. He saw a man from Maquoketa digging in an "Indian mow" (mound) beside the road. **Not found.**
- **Side item (FOUND):** "down on the **Green Island bottoms**" (Jackson Co., Mississippi bottoms) "a fellar was choppin' down a rotten tree and he found **$28,000 in gold**". Hearsay.
- **Place:** the old Indian trail from Andrew toward the iron bridge (over the Maquoketa?) along **Brush Creek**, with Indian mounds by the road. Cross-ref: Appendix fn.78.

## State Center Gold: Marshall County, "A Stage Coach and an Enlistment" (pp.57-61)
- **Informants:** John White (debate coach, Council Bluffs H.S.) and his father (letter; the father came to the area in 1876); **Charles Flora of Rhodes, Ia.** (landowner); **Floyd Roberts**, a young farmer to the east, who also owns the land.
- **Story versions:**
  1. An old man beset by Indians on the trail "about 8 miles south of State Center" buried a **pot of gold** (about a peck measure, "about $10,000") at the foot of an oak.
  2. During the **Civil War** the **Newton-Edenville stage** was attacked by robbers "along the **branch of the North Skunk River about one mile east and eight miles south of State Center**". A **U.S. mail sack** with "considerable money" was buried in a **kettle** by a member who then went to war and never returned.
  3. (Flora) A bank robber (**$40,000**) buried it in a kettle by **oak trees "in the first grove north of where the North Skunk joins the South Skunk"**, "right down here in our pasture" (some say the second grove). One tree was grubbed out; the stump of the other is still in the bottom.
  4. (Roberts) An old man who **robbed the bank in Newton of $50,000** came out on the stage and put it in a big iron kettle with a box over it **between two hickory trees**. He lived in a log cabin "in the grove where the barn stands now" on the Roberts grandfather's farm. On his deathbed his brother searched but **never found it**. Neighbours dug up the whole yard and barnyard.
- **Place:** the Flora/Roberts farms about 1 mi E and 8 mi S of State Center, by the North Skunk/South Skunk confluence groves (Marshall/Jasper/Story border, near Rhodes). **Not found.**

## Bellevue Treasure: Jackson County, "A Dark Villain and a Deep Hole" (pp.78-84)
- **Background:** the 1840 "Bellevue War" against **W. W. Brown**'s hotel gang (horse thieves, counterfeiters; "Banditti of the Prairies"). Sources cited: Glazier, *Down the Great River* (1893); Cole, *History of the People of Iowa* (1921), p.161; Gue, *History of Iowa* (1903), pp.332-4; Bonney, *Banditti of the Prairies* (1847), p.222.
- **Informants:** an old Bellevue resident (photo caption "Louis Efferding"; neighbours Smith and Graham), and **Hon. James Ellis of Maquoketa** (Jackson Co. historian, about 75), who gives an opposite, pro-Brown view.
- **Story:** Brown's money was never found; "folks thought he must have been pretty well fixed". Fox came back after the war, probably for money. "**Some thought it was buried in what they called the City Well, right over on that corner.** They find rock there yet, plowin'." **Not found.**
- **Place:** the "City Well" on a corner near Brown's hotel site in Bellevue. Cross-ref: Appendix fn.61.
- **Related (p.78):** the Fox gang's loot from the Col. George Davenport robbery (1845), buried near the mouth of the Des Moines River (Lee Co.), with John Long's confession directions (the "72" walnut tree). Out of region (SE Iowa).

## McGregor Gold (Fort Crawford paymaster gold): Clayton and Allamakee Counties, 4 versions (pp.18-46; HathiTrust seq 57-119)
Already on the atlas (McGregor row; see also newspapers-com/ia-il-sweep.md, Webster City Freeman 1913-10-21). These are Laird's source lines only.
- **Version 1, printed (p.18-22):** *Crawford County Press*, reprinted in the *North Iowa Times* (McGregor), **20 July 1905, p.1**. "In 1830" four bags of gold, said to be **$890,000** (locals say $80,000 or $90,000), arrived at Fort Crawford to pay the soldiers. The commander gave one bag each to four soldiers to hide from the Indians; they were killed. A scrap of paper on the body of one, **Mercierre**, said the money was buried "**in the highest point across from the place where they were found**" (i.e. the Iowa bluffs opposite Prairie du Chien). Never found. Laird cites Mahan, *Old Fort Crawford and the Frontier* (1926).
- **Version 2, "The Omnipresent Fortune of Fort Crawford" (pp.23-31):**
  - **Mrs. J. G. Laird** (Laird's mother, about 70), of the Laird farm near **Waukon Junction**: $80,000 being carried up the **old military road** from Fort Crawford to Fort Atkinson; chased, buried, all killed; a **French document** gave the distance from a certain tree. "Some said the money was buried on the **first bluff from the old road**, that would be the one **south of Yellow River**, and some said **the second bluff, that would be ours**." She first heard it from "Mrs. K." (Mrs. Vera K-, of McGregor, a writer who used the name "Walter Scott").
  - Laird's sister Marjorie: four lots of $20,000. His brother Burton: "**under a flat stone beneath an oak tree on the highest bluff directly across from Fort Crawford**."
  - **Mrs. R. L. Brown** (letter from Waukon Junction; she formerly lived on the bluff **north of Yellow River**).
  - **Mr. Jennings and "Aunt Fron"** (aged 84 and 80, now in McGregor; for 50 years their home stood "just where the old military road broke onto the hill top"; "Uncle Larry"): "supposed to be burried on my bluff"; $80,000 in a **brass kettle**, buried just before or after the top of the hill east of their place. "**Old Mis' Bowlder found a twenty dollar gold piece just below that tree in the turn of the road just below my place**" (about 1908).
  - **Willis Bickel**, who owns **the bluff north of McGregor**: treasure hunters dug up his iron survey pegs.
  - A Prairie du Chien version: so much digging went on around the ruins of old Fort Crawford that it helped demolish them.
  - **Mrs. Daniel Davis** (photo, p.29-30): "My mother seen" **old (Hercules) Dousman** haul the $80,000 across the river **on the ice in a bob-sled**, covered with robes (i.e. it was found and taken).
  - Laird's comment: the river curves, so from any hill for 10 miles along the Iowa bank old Fort Crawford looks "directly opposite".
- **Version 3, "The Legend of an Indian Mission and a Resurrection" (pp.32-34):** **Orley Le Hew**, sawyer, about 55, living **1 mi SE of Waukon Junction**. Two men with $60,000-100,000 for Fort Crawford, coming from "some fort in Minnesoty", with two Indian guides, followed down the **old Indian trail "right down through Billy Nickolson's pasture and down through Sixteen and up the Dousman Coolie"**. In the **Yellow River bottoms** they buried it in their **camp kettle at the foot of a big maple tree**, with blazes, and were shot. **Claimed FOUND:** about **1904 or 1905**, early spring, a young man grubbing a big stump "about six mile **southeast of Sixteen**" struck "a big iron kittle"; Le Hew thinks it was the gold and was kept secret. Laird adds the setting: the **Winnebago (Yellow River) Mission** school of the 1832 Rock Island treaty, "a fine two-story relic a few miles above the mouth of Yellow River", now demolished for its stone.
  - **Mr. and Mrs. Charlie Carney and son Ted** (ginseng growers on 40 acres **2 mi W of Waukon Junction**): Ted has "ben in every cave along the river 'cept one"; he found "old **Buxtom**" dead at the foot of a bluff, says old **Boardmann and McGowan** called it a **robbers' cave** (caved in both ways), found "a lump a' gold 'bout the size of a walnut" below it, and a marked rod/stump "right up above this cave at **Johnson's Port**". "I honestly believe that money's in thet cave."
- **Version 4, "The Legend of an Old Man and His Coy Currency" (pp.35-46):** a young farmer, about 25, "living **just north of Girard**" (Giard Twp, Clayton Co; name withheld). Sources: Price, *History of Clayton County* (1906), "Giard Township"; *Dubuque Herald*, 12 Mar 1922, p.2. The **old military road** to Fort Atkinson (a hedge row and swale; "the new road follows the ridge"), the **Quarter Way House** on the edge of the **Spanish Grant**. The "**old guv'ment barn**" (now timbers over a grey stone foundation on the hillside by the old road), kept by **Pike**. A sick old man with a heavy suitcase went out at night and came back with it light; he died; papers showed about **$50,000** lost going to Fort Atkinson; "people say its buried in a **keg**" (a keg was missing from the barn). Dug "mostly up through **those woods west**" of the barn. A diviner with a rod led them **¾ mile W across the road and down through the timber to the hollow**; they dug, found nothing. **Charlotte Esther Meyer of Girard**: government agents once dug holes, found nothing. Willis Bickel's tale: **Peter Farley** (employer of "Uncle Dan" **Daniel Bickel**) dug at night by the old barn and found a buried immigrant child. Laird: Lt. Col. L. S. Morey, War Dept finance (file 314.7/74618, 7 Apr 1927), found **no record of any lost sum** sent to Fort Crawford.
- **Places to pin (all not yet pinned):** the Laird farm bluff (second bluff from the old road, N side of Yellow River near Waukon Junction); Bickel bluff N of McGregor; the "government barn" site on the old military road near Girard; "Sixteen" and the Dousman Coulee; Johnson's Port cave (Mississippi bluff, Allamakee/Clayton). Needs a plat read before any pin.

## New Albin Gold / Black Hawk's Plunder: Allamakee County (pp.46-50; seq 119-127)
- Laird: "quite as little foundation" for the New Albin tale, "some thirty miles to the north".
- **Informant:** **Mrs. Dora Timmerman of New Albin**, a widow of German descent, middle-aged, raised on a farm **about 6 mi W of New Albin**.
- **Story:** "gold and jewelries" of **Black Hawk** (1832 war). Her father pointed out a light that flashed up in the yard at night ("gold will do that way when it lays in the ground a long time"); they hunted for it by day too and never found the place. Black Hawk (in her version) "swim across here and went out toward our farm" (then all woods, about ½ mile), was seen watering his horse, and was shot; the battle was "right across the river" (the **Bad Axe**, 1832, opposite New Albin). She has a piece of the "Black Hawk tree" from Prairie du Chien. Cross-ref "A Legend of Divination", Appendix fn.44-62.
- **Place:** the Timmerman home farm, about 6 mi W of New Albin. Not found.
