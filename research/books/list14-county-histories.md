## 16. Jackson Co IA: *History of Jackson County, Iowa* (Western Hist. Co., 1879; archive.org historyofjackson00west) and Ellis, *History of Jackson County* (1910, vol. 1; historyofjackson01elli_0)
- **No "pest house", no "City Well"**, and no buried or hidden money passage tied to W. W. Brown in either book (searches: pest house; City Well; buried/hidden/secreted + money/gold; treasure).
- The 1879 history (pp.372-374) does give the **Bellevue-era counterfeiters' "mint"**: a small cabin "in a hollow in the 'Big Timber'" in a ravine running down to the **Wapsipinicon** ("Wapsie"), about 4 mi beyond a river crossing where Indians ferried the posse. A forge and bellows were inside; "One-Thumbed Thompson" was linked to it. Deputy James T. Hanby later led a raid from Bellevue via Canton. Earlier pages (about p.369-370) cover Brown's gang ("a gang of thieves and counterfeiters at Brown's") and a man caught at Galena with **$8,000 in counterfeit** sewn in his collar. This is context only, not a cache.

## 18. Columbia Co WI: *History of Columbia County* (Butterfield, 1880; historyofcolumbi00butt), Pauquette
- **Death:** Pierre (Peter) Pauquette was shot on the evening of **Monday 17 Oct 1836** by **Man-ze-mon-e-ka**, a son of Whirling Thunder, after a quarrel at the Winnebago treaty council (pp.499-500, an eyewitness letter; *Galena Advertiser* 22 Oct 1836). The shooting happened N of Paul Grignon's house, near the narrator's house (the former Francis Le Roy house, near O. P. Williams'), close to the portage and the old American Fur Co. post "near the grist-mill". The killer's lodge was "near where Armstrong's brickyard now is".
- **Estate (p.507):** "Pauquette had purchased a number of sections of land from half-breeds, besides three sections which were given him by the Indians under their treaties, so that, at his death, he had **twelve or sixteen sections of land, a large number of cattle and horses**, together with a fine store of Indian goods". The fur company sold the goods. "**In 1838, the company received the $22,000 which the Indians owed Pauquette** at his death." The lands were sold by the administrator, and "**all this property disappeared without the heirs receiving a cent of it**."
- **Grave:** he was buried under the floor of the unfinished log Catholic church he had built "near the center of what is now Portage City"; it later burned. The lot was owned by **Benjamin L. Webb** (proprietor of Portage, "originally a French grant of 640 acres, granted to … Lecuyer"). In **1852** the grading of **Adams Street** went through the graveyard, so the bones were moved and reburied "beneath the threshold of the south entrance" of the frame church at the **corner of Conant and Adams streets** (later the Catholic parochial school). Decayed log-house foundations, charcoal and ashes were found near the grave (p.~600).
- **Farm:** "Pauquette's farm at **Belle Fontaine**" is mentioned (about p.430). Belle Fontaine is the spring/settlement area NE of Portage toward Pardeeville (not located here).
- **1873 Columbia Co atlas (Harrison & Warner; Ancestry 1127, 41 images), one look:** the **Fort Winnebago Twp plate (T13N R9E, atlas p.50) = viewer p.32, image 31617_072126-00749**: Port Hope, the Fox River, owners Stauber, Prentice, Moran, Whitney, Gamble, O'Keefe, Carroll, Cushing, Sanborn, Tallman, Wells, Sweeny, Murphy and others; "Fort Winnebago" is at the S edge. **No Pauquette / Paquette tract** and no "Belle Fontaine" label seen. The Portage city plate (viewer p.24) was not searched. There is no 1861 atlas in 1127.
- **Placement:** the grave is at **Conant & Adams Sts, Portage** (city centre, N of the canal). This is a named landmark for the Pauquette stop, grade B (reburial site, 1852).
