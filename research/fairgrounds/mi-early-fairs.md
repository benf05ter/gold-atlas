### Batch 2 candidates (not yet sent)
- **Coldwater (Branch) 1858**: "Branch County Agricultural Grounds" lettered N of the village, E side of the road running NNW out of Grand St (today N. Grand / old US-27), about 290 m N of the dashed section line (Geil & Jones *Map of Branch County* 1858, Coldwater inset, LoC 2012593022, full-res region x1530-5480 y250-3950). No outline drawn, so the label spot only: ~41.9589, -85.0098 (measured against USGS Coldwater West 1960 1:24,000 street grid). Conf C (~250 m). Needs a plat (1872/1877 Branch atlas) to outline.
- Checked, no fair ground drawn: Romeo 1859 inset (Macomb & St. Clair map) - the ground came later; Hillsdale Village 1857 inset (partial view); Marshall 1858 inset (west half only); Bennington Twp 1859 (Hartwellville).

- **Howell (Livingston) 1860-c.1890s**, PLACED: Standard Atlas of Livingston Co. 1895 (Ogle), "East Part of Howell" pp.46-47 (LoC 2007633239, image ca000023), draws NO fair ground by 1895. It does draw T. W. Mizner's Washington Heights Addition in E1/2 SE1/4 sec. 36, with **FAIR AVE** on its west side, and B. H. Rubert's 42 1/2 acres between the addition and the Howell/Oceola line, north of Grand River Ave. Taken with the Livingston Republican 1875-08-18 (Mizner offers to trade "the present grounds for more land further east") and the 1880 history (Washington Heights "near the fair-grounds"; ~20 acres in 1876 after "the last addition"): the pre-1875 grounds lay on or beside Fair Ave / Washington Heights, and the 1875-on grounds lay just east, on the Rubert land. Gone by 1895, and USGS Howell 1907 draws nothing. Pin at the centre of the Rubert block N of Grand River: **42.6041, -83.9159**. Section corners are from BLM PLSS (sec. 36 T3N R4E SE corner 42.60026, -83.91436); the plat was measured from its "Center Section 36" and township-corner circles. Conf C (~250 m; covers both phases). East-side Howell today: probably built up, look-only unless open ground.
- Brighton: the 1895 atlas Brighton village plat is on p.50, which is not in the LoC scan set (only the right margin shows on p.51). Still open.

- **Corunna (Shiawassee) 1850-1858**, PLACED: the 1859 Geil & Jones Genesee & Shiawassee map (LoC 2011588008), "Corunna Village" inset, letters a full block **"Fair Ground"** in Castle's Addition, south of David St and north of King St. That is two blocks east of the river's southward loop and across the river from the mill yard. The 1880 history says the grounds were "on the south side of the river, prepared by the citizens"; this block is south of the river's northern reach, so it fits. Transferred to USGS Corunna 1926 1:62,500 by the river loop and the N-S main road (Shiawassee St) crossing: **42.9840, -84.1154**. Conf C (~200 m). The 1926 sheet shows this strip as open ground between the grid and the race line. Edge of the city today, look-only unless public.
- Brighton: newspapers.com cannot place the 1875 grounds (Brighton papers not indexed). The 1895 atlas Brighton page is missing on LoC. Still open; next try the 1859 Ingham & Livingston map inset (LoC 2012593015), for the 1853-59 grounds.

### Described in period text, not yet placed
- **Shiawassee: Corunna, then Hartwellville, then Owosso.** 1850-1858 fairs at Corunna "on grounds situated on the
  south side of the river, prepared by the citizens" (History of Shiawassee and Clinton Cos., 1880, p.140,
  archive.org historyofshiawas00elli). The 1853 fair was at **Hartwellville** (clipping 1); the 1880 history does
  not mention it. Hartwellville = J. M. Hartwell's tavern/P.O. on the Grand River road, E edge of Bennington Twp
  sec. 24/13 (1859 Geil & Jones Genesee-Shiawassee map, LoC 2011588008, shows J.M. Hartwell land and P.O. there;
  no fair ground drawn). New association 1860: grounds "on Hickory Street", Owosso, 6+ acres, board fence,
  80-rod track, used 1860-1879; 1877 bought the 30-acre "Eggleston tract"; buildings moved there spring 1880,
  first fair on new grounds fall 1880 (same book p.140-141). Hickory St is in Owosso's east ward on the 1859
  city inset. **Owosso Hickory St grounds 1860-79 = urban, look-only. Corunna 1850-58 = south of the river, spot
  not given.**
- **Livingston: Brighton (1853, 1858, 1859), Howell public square (1854-57), Howell permanent 1860 on.**
  1853 fair at Brighton, citizens paid $168.41 "preparing Fair-Ground" (spot not given). 1854 fair on the public
  square in Howell. 1858 and 1859 at Brighton (1859: citizens enclosed a ground and put up buildings). March 1860:
  6 acres "near the toll-gate, east of the village of Howell"; first fair there Sept 26-28 1860; grown to ~20 acres
  with a half-mile track by 1876, "on the north side of Grand River Street, a short distance east of the compact
  portion of the village ... within the corporation"; Mizner's Washington Heights Addition (E1/2 SE1/4 sec. 36)
  lies "north of Grand River Street, near the fair-grounds"; Moses Thompson's first house was "directly east of
  the ... fair-grounds", near the S end of Thompson Lake (History of Livingston Co., 1880, pp.126-128, 133, 152,
  archive.org historylivingst00unkngoog). Brighton had "adjoining fair-grounds" used by the gun club in 1877
  (p.515). USGS Howell 1907 draws no track. **Howell 1860 grounds: N of Grand River Ave, E side of town, sec. 36
  T3N R4E, likely by the S end of Thompson Lake; needs a plat or Sanborn to pin.**
- **Macomb: Utica 1850, Romeo later.** First fair at Utica Sept 1850 "opposite the site of the Congregational
  Church", no fences or track; society reorganized 1857; fairs at Romeo in the 1860s-1873, Mt. Clemens 1874
  (History of Macomb Co., 1882, pp.473-474 and chronology, archive.org historyofmacombc00lees). Romeo's
  fair-grounds sit "near or on the site" of Albert Finch's 1823-24 home at Indian Village (Romeo's first name),
  and Squires and Smith settled "half a mile north of the fair grounds" (pp. 1350k, 1842k in the OCR).
  Spot not given; Indian Village + 1820s settlers + fair = strong site if placed.
- **Clinton: St. Johns.** (now PLACED above) 1866 bought 30 acres of Joseph Cardinal "one mile south of the village"; judges' stand
  1879 (History of Shiawassee and Clinton Cos., 1880, Clinton Co. agricultural society section). Also the Central Fair (Ionia, Montcalm,
  Gratiot, Clinton) grounds, ~30 acres, sec. 18 Lebanon Twp, just E of Hubbardston (same book). Both placeable
  from PLSS + 1873 Clinton atlas (LoC 2010587156).
- **Berrien: Niles.** Fairs at Niles from 1850s except 1868; March 1858 bought R. C. Paine's land "west of the
  cemetery", 5+ acres, $725; fenced, buildings and graded track 1858 (History of Berrien and Van Buren Cos.,
  1880, archive.org historyofberrien00ensi). The 1868 Ruger bird's-eye of Niles (LoC 73693441) draws the Fair
  Ground (key no. 11) as an oval at the east edge of town. Urban now, probably look-only; to pin.
- **Cass: Cassopolis.** USGS Cassopolis 1945 draws a race track NE of the village in sec. 25 near
  ~41.920, -86.005 (to be measured properly). 1882 Cass history mentions fair grounds at Cassopolis only in passing.
- **Gratiot: Ithaca.** USGS Alma 1935 draws a race track at the SW edge of Ithaca by the railroad, ~43.287, -84.611
  (to be measured properly). The 1869 grounds are not yet described in text.
- **Jackson:** "Jackson County Fair Grounds" include land Thomas Cuff bought by the old prison "Tamaracks"
  (Portrait & Biog. Album, archive.org). Urban.
- **Kalamazoo:** the fair-grounds stand on land entered in 1832 by a pioneer guide (Berrien/Van Buren history
  bio). Urban.

  blocked data node).

- Blocked cloud-side this session: basemap.nationalmap.gov, Esri basemaps, TIGERweb, Michigan state GIS
  (gisagocss.state.mi.us), services5.arcgis.com, nominatim. So modern street positions must come from topo grids

## PC results (ask list 1)
- A1 Hartwellville: Detroit Free Press 1853-09-14 p2 (newspapers.com img 1049358287), "County Agricultural Fairs" from the Adrian Watch Tower: "Shiawassee, Oct 6-7, Hartwellville". Town only, no grounds. Confirms clipping 1. Still unplaced.
- A2 Romeo: Macomb papers are not indexed on newspapers.com for 1858-80. Unresolved.
- A3 Owosso: Owosso Times 1882-09-22 p5 (img 174184278): "the barn of H. F. Eggleston, a mile north of this city". So the 1877 "Eggleston tract" (the 1880 fairground) is probably about 1 mi N of Owosso. The USGS Chesaning 1917 sheet draws no track N of Owosso. PC (a) is searching the Owosso Times 1878-81 next.
- Batch 1 (5 pins) is in app PR #164 (Ground check).
- A3 Owosso (more): Owosso Times 1881-83 gives 13 hits for "fair grounds Hickory", which may mean the post-1880 grounds were also reached by Hickory St (it may run N toward the Eggleston place). Two hits read so far were noise; 1882-10-06 p5 is next.
- A4 Howell: Livingston Republican 1866-04-18 p3 (img 453499222): a stock company formed to build a race track "in the immediate vicinity of the Fair Grounds". Fits the 1866 lease to the Livingston County Horse Association (1880 history p.133). No street named.
- A5 Brighton: Livingston Republican 1875-10-06 p1 (img 453117608), quoting the Brighton Citizen 1875-09-30: Hart, Conely & Co. are to supply 60,000+ ft of lumber "for the fair grounds", for buildings and fences. So Brighton built NEW grounds in fall 1875. These are the "adjoining fair-grounds" the Brighton Gun Club used in 1877 (1880 history p.515), which places them next to the club's grounds. The 1853/1858/1859 Brighton sites are separate and still unknown.
- A7 Niles: Niles Republican 1859-01-27 p2 (img 1019944710) prints the Berrien Co. Agricultural Society statement for 1858: $725 from citizens "towards purchase and fitting up of Fair Grounds", fencing, and "grubbing, ditching, excavating lake, draining, filling in, grading track" ($314). So the grounds had a DUG POND and a graded track, which could still show on 1938 aerials or lidar. The 1859 Geil Harley & Siverd "City of Niles" inset (LoC 2012593021) draws the Cemetery at the SE edge (Maple Lane, beside C. K. Green / Hoffman's Addition) but NO fair ground. The 1868 Ruger bird's-eye draws the Fair Ground (key 11) as an oval at the E edge of town. Search area: E/SE Niles, west of the cemetery. Not pinned.
- Cassopolis: the 1859 Cassopolis inset (same map) draws no fair ground.
- A7 Niles (more): Niles Democrat 1859-10-08 p2 (img 1102001407), quoting the Michigan City Enterprise, describes "a fine oval lake of living water" in the centre of the grounds, with a carriage way around it and ground rising on every side. USGS Cassopolis 1945 1:62,500 (278031) draws a closed, N-S elongated contour depression inside the east-side street grid, centred ~41.8298, -86.2401, about 0.5 km N of the cemetery symbol. It is a CANDIDATE for the filled pond bowl, not proven (the 1858 text says "west of the cemetery"). Lidar check wanted. Not pinned.
- **Niles 1858-1860s, PLACED (conf C)**. Niles Democrat 1860-12-08 p3 (img 1063976855): a petition to open TENTH ST "to the fair ground". On the 1859 City of Niles inset (LoC 2012593021), Tenth St runs N-S through Beeson's Addition from Howard St to Broadway. Extended south past Hickory and Maple Lanes, it reaches the ground W of the Cemetery at the SE corner of the plat, which fits the 1858 purchase "west of the cemetery" (1880 history). Measured from the inset (river at Main St to Tenth ~945 m; ~1.6 m per inset px from block spacing) and corrected to the cemetery symbol on USGS Cassopolis 1945 (~41.8217, -86.2421). Pin: **41.8220, -86.2455**, a 300 m circle on open ground W of the cemetery on the 1945 sheet. The earlier depression candidate (41.8298, -86.2401) lies E of the Tenth St line and among platted 1859 lots, so it is DISCOUNTED. Physical clue: oval spring-fed pond in the centre, track around it (1858 accounts; 1859 description).
- A10 Cassopolis: newspapers.com gives no site words (the 1859 Cass County Weekly and 1868 National Democrat hits are premium lists and rules only). Stays as the 1945 track pin, grade D.
- State Board reports 1869/1871 (PC (b); detail in research/fairgrounds/mi-state-ag-society-transactions.md):

The 1945 NE track is consistent with the 1869 replacement grounds (inside 3/4 mi) but not proven. Pin stays D.
  - Gratiot: no show-ground of its own in 1869, so the Oct 1869 Ithaca fair was on borrowed or temporary ground. The 1935 SW track is a later ground.
  - Owosso 1871, Romeo, Traverse City: fairs held, no sites named.
