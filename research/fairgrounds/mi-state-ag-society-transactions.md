## B1. 1853 volume: v.5 (1853), HathiTrust **mdp.39015071508736** (also nyp.33433007598141, chi.096867539)
- Full-text search across the series: "Hartwellville" appears only in v.3 (1851) and v.5 (1853).
- **Shiawassee (Hartwellville):**
  - p.621 (#637): "LIST OF PREMIUMS Awarded at the 4th Annual Fair, of the Shiawasse County Agricultural Society, held at Hartwellville, on the 6th and 7th days of October, 1853". Prize winners include A. C. Botsford (Byron), G. Hickey (Bennington) and several Hartwells of Bennington.
  - p.625 (#641): an address "delivered before the Shiawassee County Agricultural and Horticultural Society, at Hartwellville" by L. H. Parsons, Esq.
  - **No ground, lot or farm is named for the Hartwellville fair** in pp.617-625. The report says only "held at Hartwellville".
- **Livingston (Brighton):** pp.483-484 (#499-500), report dated "Howell, December 15, 1853". The society was organized at Howell on 24 Feb 1853. The first fair was to go to whichever village or township raised the most money, so it was held "**at the village of Brighton on the 6th and 7th days of October**", Brighton having pledged $140. On 5 Sept, **Wm. R. Cobb, Esq., of Brighton** was appointed Marshal "to prepare the Fair ground, enclose the same with a suitable fence, and erect the necessary pens, sheds &c." p.503 (#519) accounts: "Paid for lumber and expenses of preparing Fair ground, $168 41"; W. R. Cobb paid $37.13 as Marshal. p.443 (#459): the town that raises the most by subscription within six months "for the purpose of fitting up a fair ground" gets the next County Fair. **The Brighton site is not described** (no owner or lot).
- **Macomb:** no Macomb County society report in v.5. Macomb appears only as exhibitors' home county at the State Fair (e.g. p.37, P. K. Leach, Utica).
- **St. Joseph:** no county society report in v.5. "St. Joseph County" hits are only the 1854 state census tables (pp.1009-1012).

## B2/B3: *Annual Report of the Secretary of the State Board of Agriculture* (county society reports)
- **1869 report** (miun.aaf7919.1869.001; county reports pp.366-400, seq 376-412):
  - **Cass** (p.369, D. M. Howell, Cassopolis): "Our Society sold the old show grounds in consequence of the Peninsular Railway passing through them, and has purchased **thirty acres within three-fourths of a mile of Cassopolis**"; the last fair was held with "no grounds except an open field".
  - **Gratiot** (about p.386, Cummings): "the Society labors under the disadvantage of **owning no show-ground**", so exhibitions are open and free except the Hall. **So there was no fixed Ithaca ground in 1869.**
  - **Grand Traverse** (about p.383): the Second Annual Exhibition was held at Traverse City, 6-7 Oct (no ground described).
  - **Livingston** (p.399, A. Tooley of Genoa): prosperous; "a new Hall"; the society will soon be "well situated in respect to grounds, buildings" (no site named).
  - **Macomb** (p.400, Briggs): the 20th annual exhibition was held at **Romeo**, 29 Sep-1 Oct (no site named).
  - Others for context: Ingham at Lansing (about 40 acres); Eaton sold its old 8-acre ground and bought **26 acres about ¾ mi S of Charlotte**; St. Joseph on "the grounds of the Society, at Centreville".
  - **Clinton, Montcalm, Shiawassee: no report** in that volume.
- **1871 report** (uc1.a0002920411; county reports about seq 415-470):
  - **Cass** (p.402-403): "compelled to vacate those well improved **grounds south of the village** in consequence of the Peninsular Railway passing through it." So the pre-1870 Cassopolis ground lay S of the village on the railroad line.
  - **Shiawassee** (p.433-434): the 12th annual fair of the Shiawassee County Agricultural Association was held at **Owosso**, 4-6 Oct 1871; 939 entries, receipts $1,152. No street or Hickory St site named.
  - Livingston (p.423): a drought report, no site.
  - Clinton, Gratiot, Montcalm, Grand Traverse, Macomb: no report found in seq 415-475.
- The 1866 report (miun.aaf7919.0003.001) has Ithaca, St. Johns and Owosso only in farm essays, not county fair reports.
- **Takeaway:** these reports rarely give a fair-ground location. The best lines are Cassopolis (old ground S of the village on the Peninsular Ry; a new 30 acres within ¾ mi, 1869) and Gratiot (no ground in 1869).

## Cassopolis: 1872 Lake atlas of Cass Co (Ancestry 1127, Cass 1872, D. J. Lake, Philadelphia)
- The Cassopolis village plan is split across **viewer p.20 (bottom inset, image 31617_072119-00653)** and **viewer p.21 (…-00654)**, with the "Cassopolis Business Directory" below.
- **No "Fair Ground" label in 1872.**
  - N edge (p.20): small acreages (R. E. Graham, M. J. Graham, J. E. Peck, W. P. Bennett, S. T. Read 2½a, Union School, J. D. Bonine brick kiln, J. A. Reynolds slaughter house); Saml. Graham 31a and Moses McIlvain 11½a to the NE; Henry Bloodgood 52a (Lagrange Twp) on the E edge; Maj. Jos. Smith 18a on the W.
  - S part (p.21): Stone Lake on the W; streets State, Jefferson, Water, South; the **Peninsular Railway** and the **Michigan Air Line** cross diagonally through the SE quarter, meeting near Sherman St and the Depot. SE of the railways are open tracts **E. B. Sherman 20a and 5a** and **S. T. Read 18a and 4a**.
- **Reading:** the 1869-70 report says the old show ground was "south of the village" and the Peninsular Ry passed through it. By 1872 that land is the open E. B. Sherman / S. T. Read acreage SE of the village where the Peninsular line runs (unlabelled). The new 30-acre ground "within ¾ mile" is outside the village plan and not shown. **Candidate (old ground): the Sherman/Read tracts SE of South St along the Peninsular Ry, grade C.**
