# C. Y. Reed safe robbery, Oconomowoc (Waukesha Co. WI): newspapers.com check (2026-10-31)

Asked for by the treasure thread via the coordinator. Source to check: History of Waukesha County (1880) pp.700-701, which dates it "c.1855", with $1,000-1,500 taken, a clairvoyant, a hollow tree, a can sunk in the lake, and the lake dragged.
- leads.tsv / negatives.tsv: no prior row (Oconomowoc appears only in a Find a Grave cemetery list).

## Result: the robbery was in SEPTEMBER 1859, not c.1855
Searches for 1854-57 (Reed + Oconomowoc + safe/robbed) gave only Herbert Reed's Milwaukee store ads ("Reed's Notices") and noise. The real story is in 1859.

1. **Oconomowoc Free Press, 29 Sept 1859**, as quoted in the **Waukesha County Democrat, 4 Oct 1859, p2**, "Daring Robbery": https://www.newspapers.com/image/1064946068/
   - "Last night [27/28 Sept 1859] the store of C. Y. Reed was entered by some person or persons, and money to the amount $1,600 taken from out the safe."
   - Reed had the store and safe keys in his pocket and left his pants at the foot of his bed. In the morning he found them in the wood shed with the keys gone; the store was open and the safe robbed.
2. **Oconomowoc Free Press, 20 Oct 1859, p3**, "Interesting Items -- Police tact -- Mesmerism -- Progress of Science -- Terrible Excitement": https://www.newspapers.com/image/1095980600/
   - "On the morning of Sept. 28, Mr. C. Y. Reed alleges that his store was broken into, the safe entered, and the sum of $1,600... taken out."
   - Young men leaving town were told they "would be examined"; one took or offered to take his valise to Reed's to be searched.
   - A "celebrated clairvoyant" was brought in "last Saturday" [15 Oct 1859]. With Mr. Reed, ex-District Attorney C. W. Putnam and A. Harrison, Esq., he "crawled through the cellar window in the school house, went up into the second story" for a seance (rye whiskey, three raps under Dan. Webster's seat).
   - The clairvoyant said "the money had been stolen, A. & B. stole the money, and a POC-MARKED MAN HID IT IN THE LAKE IN 12 FEET OF WATER."
   - A steam whistle interrupted the session. On Monday night [17 Oct] he tried again and said "the money has been removed."
   - The editor is openly sceptical of the "alleged robbery" ("All sorts of charges have been made without the slightest evidence... there is but one opinion among our citizens... we don't believe everything we hear"). This hints that townspeople suspected an inside job or Reed himself.
3. **Oconomowoc Free Press, 16 Feb 1860, p3**, "Reward": "Our citizens have offered a reward of $500 to learn something positive in relation to the robbery of C. Y. Reed's store." So it was still unsolved in Feb 1860. https://www.newspapers.com/image/1095983052/
4. **Oconomowoc Free Press, 15 Nov 1860, p3**: a dealer's ad, "the stock of goods formerly owned by C. Y. Reed". Someone "purchased the entire stock... in the store formerly occupied by C. Y. Reed". So Reed's store was sold by Nov 1860. https://www.newspapers.com/image/1095987002/
5. Context: Waukesha Freeman, 1 Nov 1859, p2, "A part of Mr. Worthington's Record and the whole of Mr. Reed's Record". C. Y. Reed was an Assembly candidate in the fall 1859 election (political, not about the robbery). https://www.newspapers.com/image/1065100541/

## Answers
- **Date:** night of 27-28 Sept 1859, from Reed's store safe in Oconomowoc village.
- **Amount:** $1,600.
- **Arrests:** none reported through Feb 1860. A $500 citizens' reward was posted in Feb 1860.
- **Recovered:** no recovery found in these papers. By Feb 1860 there was still no "positive" information.
- **Hiding place named:** only by the clairvoyant: "hid it in the Lake in 12 feet of water", with no lake named. Oconomowoc village sits on Fowler Lake and Lac La Belle, and Oconomowoc Lake is just E. Then "the money has been removed". The hollow tree and the can version, and the lake dragging, come from the 1880 county history. They were not seen in the 1859 papers read (the Free Press for 22 Sept - 12 Oct and Oct 1859 - Feb 1860 was not read issue by issue).
- **Grade:** D. A doubtful robbery, possibly an inside job; the only "location" is a seance claim of an unnamed lake.

## Not read
- The Free Press original of 29 Sept 1859 (the issue may not be on the site).
- The 27 Oct 1859 to Jan 1860 issues, which may cover the lake dragging.
- The Watertown papers.
