## 1. VEJVODA / VAJVODA (Barron Co WI)
- Eau Claire Leader-Telegram 1925-10-10 p7, "Farmer Near Rice Lake Recovers $6,000 in Hectic Search" (Rice Lake special): https://www.newspapers.com/image/270709261/
  - "...found about $6,000 so far, on the farm owned by himself and wife, NORTHEAST OF RICE LAKE. John Vejvoda and wife came from Bohemia thirty years ago, and 28 YEARS AGO [c.1897] BOUGHT A PIECE OF LAND and made a farm of it... the wife acted as banker, and buried and hid the money. Mrs. Vejvoda died in July and her husband had a public sale... Some was found in a flour sack, some in a sack of poppy seed, and some buried. Over $1,000 was found in an old coat, but the mice had destroyed it... corners of $20 bills. Mr. Vejvoda is now depositing his cash in the local banks. He has an idea that there is A LARGE QUANTITY OF SILVER BURIED BETWEEN THE ROAD AND THE HOUSE."
- Later search "Vejvoda" + "Rice Lake" 1925-1935: only the Oct 1925 cluster (+ unrelated Nebraska Vejvodas). No report the silver was ever dug up. Wife's first name not given. Road/town not named beyond NE of Rice Lake.
- Pin hint: the strip between the road and the farmhouse on the John Vejvoda farm NE of Rice Lake (1897-1925 owner; plat check).

## 4. DICKSON / DIXON (Palmyra, Warren Co IA)
- Des Moines Register 1909-10-31 p25 (Sunday feature, full page): https://www.newspapers.com/image/128404303/
  - PLACE: "The home of Andy Dickson is located about TEN MILES NORTHEAST OF INDIANOLA, and TWO MILES NORTHWEST OF PALMYRA... 100-acre farm" (house set back from the road among elm and maple trees, with "ten or a dozen outhouses, some of them whitewashed"). "In Mr. Dickson's latter days, one of these little houses was ALWAYS KEPT PADLOCKED, and it was here that THE BOX CONTAINING THE GOLD WAS DUG FROM BENEATH THE FLOOR."
  - "By order of Judge Nichols of Perry, a search of Andy Dickson's premises brought to light $13,300 in shining twenty, ten, five and one dollar gold pieces." Searchers pictured: Sheriff Grant Kimer, Supervisor Lee Hiatt. Administrator: FRANK SMITH, nephew of Mrs. Dickson. Widow (Mrs. Dickson, née HARRIET FLESHER, ~70) survived; he died AUG 20 [1909]: "The night before he died, a relative suggested... reveal... the secret hiding place. 'I have lots and lots of money,' the dying man replied, 'but I would have to show you where it is. We will look for it tomorrow.'" Buried in the little cemetery near CARLISLE.
  - Bio: b. North Carolina 1828; came to Iowa at 16; Quaker; settled near Palmyra ~1851-52; blacksmith in Palmyra village ~25 yrs before; land trader; neighbors believed he buried money on the farm; he once paid >$3,000 in gold from overcoat pockets at Sen. W.H. Berry's office, Indianola.
- Not found in this read: heirs' farm sale or second find (unread: DM Register 1909-09-19 p1, Indianola papers).

## 6. OLESEN (Lynn, Clark Co WI)
- Dodge County Citizen 1900-11-08 p1, "Old Crime Recalled: Box of Plunder Is Discovered Buried Under Tree Near Lynn" (LYNN, Wis., Nov. 5 - Special): https://www.newspapers.com/image/1036763344/ (same special in ~12 WI papers Nov 6-15, 1900: Kenosha 11/6, Ozaukee/West Bend 11/7, Waupun & Mining Times 11/9, Oconomowoc & Daily Tribune 11/10, Darlington 11/15).
  - "While engaged in cutting down a MAPLE TREE which grew IN HIS PASTURE, ANDREW OLESEN discovered a cavity near its base, and... found a wooden box about two feet square filled with plunder... 120 silver dollars of both American and Mexican mintage, dates 1846 to 1859. About $200 in gold coin... eagles and double eagles; several pounds of old gold and silver watch cases; candlesticks and... solid silver knives, forks and spoons."
  - "This discovery recalls... some TWENTY YEARS AGO [c.1880] a well-to-do farmer living BETWEEN HERE [Lynn] AND ROMADKA, THE NEXT STATION WEST, was supposed to have been murdered and his home robbed and burned. Last year [1899] a skeleton... was discovered buried by the ROADSIDE, NEAR BY WHERE OLESEN FOUND the buried property. A neighbor of the missing farmer was suspected... Later... caught burglarizing a store at PITTSVILLE... serving a twenty-year sentence in Waupun."
- Result: date Nov 1900 (find ~Nov 1-5), finder ANDREW OLESEN, maple in his pasture, between Lynn and Romadka (railroad station W of Lynn), near the roadside where an 1899 skeleton was found. RECOVERED (box found). Plat target: Andrew Olesen W of Lynn toward Romadka.

## 7. MARCUS IL 1902 Burlington holdup - trial
- Rock Island Argus 1903-07-10 p6, "Men on Trial for Marcus Train Robbery Convicted": "The trial of ED ESTELLE and TOM CLARK, the men held at Mount Carroll, Ill., for the robbery of a Burlington train at Marcus Aug. 5 of last year, resulted yesterday in a verdict of guilty. The trial occupied 19 days and was desperately contested... it appears that there is considerable mystery about it still." https://www.newspapers.com/image/174679699/
- Waupun Leader 1903-07-15 p6, "Gordon Told His Pals to Kill Him Rather Than Leave Him When Wounded" (La Crosse, July 11): Estelle's confession - he (not express messenger Byle, who got $1000 reward) killed the wounded bandit GORDON; Gordon & Estelle had killed a Leavenworth guard and escaped; Gordon thrown off the engine "thinking he fell into the river. Instead he fell by the right of way"; a pal "BROWNIE" (later killed in a Galveston bank robbery) proposed burning him in the firebox. "The robbers were traced to Memphis and captured." https://www.newspapers.com/image/1057912802/
- RECOVERY: neither article says the ~$2,200 silver was recovered (no mention of the loot). Remains D/unresolved; "considerable mystery about it still."

## 8. SCHMIDT (Winona, 653 W 3rd St)
- Winona Daily News 1934-03-01 p3, "$1,000 Judgment Ordered in Sequel To Buried Gold Case Tried Last Fall: Niece of Louis Schmidt Required to Turn Back 'Entrusted' Money": https://www.newspapers.com/image/546230491/
  - Default judgment $1,027 vs Mrs. TOM PADGETT (Cedar Rapids IA; = Mrs. Emma McKenzie), niece of Louis Schmidt, 653 West Third St, for the First National Bank as guardian. Companion sued: JACOB WALETZKE.
  - KEY: "in the first few days he was in the hospital here, according to testimony in the case, ABOUT $14,000 WAS DUG UP IN THE SCHMIDT BASEMENT" (of the alleged $14,800). Schmidt (61, eccentric bachelor) told her and Alfred Priewert to dig it up so he'd keep control if hospitalized. Section "Buried in Ch[icken house?]" continues.
- Result: ~$14,000 of $14,800 was dug up (much later disputed/withheld by relatives, not still buried). Suggest F (recovered), small residual. Other later hits (1938-45) unread.
- SCHMIDT "Buried in Churchyard" subhead (same page): Alfred Priewert had $7,005 for a time, "but turned over $4,395 to Mrs. Padgett, who got Waletzke to help her take it to Wisconsin, where IT WAS BURIED IN A STABLE AT A COUNTRY CHURCHYARD NEAR INDEPENDENCE, WIS. Mrs. Padgett and Waletzke said they had never counted the money, but HAD DUG IT UP AGAIN, left it in a suitcase in a parcel locker at La Crosse... then in a safe at Galesville, and then turned back $2,770 to the First National bank". Priewert also dug up "a jar containing $1,000" at Schmidt's instruction. "Used Maps": "The first of several jars dug up was found without aid of a map, but Schmidt furnished a map showing hiding places of the money later. Nearly all the money was in gold."
  - => Secondary cache site (Independence WI churchyard stable, Trempealeau Co.) was DUG UP again (recovered). Not a new open lead; story color only.
