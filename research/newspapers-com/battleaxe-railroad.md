# "Battle Axe" railroad (Iron River - Washburn, Bayfield Co WI) - newspapers.com notes, 2026-10-31

Search: "Battle Axe" + "Iron River", WI 1890-1930: 185 hits (Superior Evening Telegram, Washburn Times, etc.).

1. Washburn Times 1902-05-01 p1, "...Road is Coming to Washburn! / Trunk Line Secured for [Washburn]... To buy 'Battle-Axe.'" https://www.newspapers.com/image/769084613/
   - The "Battle-Axe" = the WASHBURN, BAYFIELD & IRON RIVER RAILROAD (Ry.), then in receivership (Receiver A.C. FROST), "about to be offered for sale under a decree of the Circuit court of the United States for the Western District of Wisconsin."
   - The NORTHERN PACIFIC made a proposition to a Washburn mass-meeting committee to buy it; Washburn had "three times turned down the [W.] Bayfield & Iron River Ry."; NP "agreed to take the [line]... between here and Iron River"; a $125,000 figure is mentioned for the main line (partial column).
   - Citizens' agreement: secure for the railway company "the right of way on the MAIN LINE of said road, one hundred feet in width at all points, BETWEEN THE VILLAGES OF WASHBURN AND IRON RIVER"; a tract for railroad yards "two thousand (2,000) feet in length and four hundred (400) feet in width... located in SECTION SIX (6), TOWNSHIP FORTY-EIGHT (48), RANGE FOUR [W]"; a deed from D.M. and A.W. McLEOD releasing claims; franchise for "the construction and operation of a railway line from the PRESENT TERMINUS of said railroad at the line of WASHINGTON AVENUE, east along EAST OMAHA STREET to the northeast line of SUPERIOR AV[enue]... opposite the deep water of [Che]quamegon bay" (Washburn).
   => Route: main line Washburn <-> Iron River (SW-NE fits the coordinator's grade candidate); Washburn terminus at Washington Ave / E. Omaha St; NP purchase/junction under negotiation 1902.
2. Superior Evening Telegram 1907-07-06 p10 (Iron River column): "Conductor Hesmer of the 'Battle Axe' has returned to his post of duty after his recent illness." https://www.newspapers.com/image/1240638428/ => line still running a train with a conductor in 1907.
3. ABANDONMENT 1922:
   - Superior Evening Telegram 1922-04-22 p7 (Washburn special) "Protest From Bayfield County Residents Recognized by Governor": "Although the NORTHERN PACIFIC Railway company received an order from the INTERSTATE COMMERCE COMMISSION permitting it to abandon and tear up the WASHBURN-IRON RIVER BRANCH of the road, a distance of 34 MILES known as the 'BATTLE AXE,' the company may not be able to carry out the provisions of the order for some time at least"; county board resolution sent to Gov. Blaine; attorney general authorized to sue in the name of the state. https://www.newspapers.com/image/1240350237/
   - Washburn Times 1922-05-04 p10 "Every Branch of State Government Was Appealed to in Battle-Axe Hearing": NP abandonment fight; ICC hearing (letters re Oct 1921 hearing) - political, no route detail. https://www.newspapers.com/image/769191128/
   - Washburn Times 1922-06-22 p1 "State Loses Skirmish in Battle Axe Case / Refuses to Revert Case to Circuit Court / Holds That U.S. Government Has Power to Determine What Railroad Shall Do in Abandonment": Judge C.Z. LUSE (Superior, federal) denied the state's motion. https://www.newspapers.com/image/769191192/
   => NP owned it by 1922 (bought c.1902); 34-mile Washburn-Iron River branch; ICC abandonment order ~early 1922 (hearing Oct 1921); state suit lost June 1922; physical removal presumably 1922-23 (not yet confirmed).

5. 2026-10-31 removal-date check (coordinator ask):
   - Superior Evening Telegram 1922-06-30 p1, "Blames Lenroot and Nelson for Battle Ax Loss": Gov. Blaine's speech at Ashland. Political. https://www.newspapers.com/image/1240717998/
   - Washburn Times 1922-07-06 p1: Blaine "shedding crocodile tears over the PROPOSED destruction of the Battle Axe railroad", and the county's "fight to prevent the tearing up of this railroad". So the track was still in place in early July 1922. https://www.newspapers.com/image/769191212/
   - Superior Evening Telegram 1922-08-23 p1: motor trucks now carry the freight "which was formerly handled by the 'Battle Axe,' a branch of the Northern Pacific, ordered to be abandoned after court proceedings in the federal court". So service had ended by Aug 1922. https://www.newspapers.com/image/1240717200/
   - "Battle Axe" in Bayfield Co papers from Jul 1922 to Dec 1924 returns only the Jul 6 1922 hit; "Battle Axe" + "taking up" gave noise. The physical removal date is NOT found. Service ended about Jul-Aug 1922, and the rails were probably lifted in late 1922 or 1923. Washburn Times coverage looks thin after mid-1922.
   - The Superior Telegram Iron River and Washburn columns, Sep 1922 to 1923, would settle it with "steel" or "rails" plus "Iron River".
6. REMOVAL DATE FOUND (2026-10-31): Superior Evening Telegram 1922-11-25 p5, "Old 'Battle Ax' Being Torn Up / Crew at Work Removing Traces of Old Line - Long Standing Fight Ended" (Washburn special), https://www.newspapers.com/image/1240993117/
   - The "Battle Ax" branch of the Northern Pacific, "extending from this city [Washburn] to Iron River, a distance of about 35 miles, is being torn up". A large crew had worked "for several days"; the road "will be dismantled to a point about NINE MILES THIS SIDE OF THE VILLAGE OF IRON RIVER". "The road from here to CODA, nine miles this side of Iron River, was abandoned last summer [1922]". The line was built in 1897 by the Washburn, Bayfield & Iron River railway company.
   - => The rails between Washburn and Coda were lifted in mid-to-late Nov 1922. Coda (about 9 mi E of Iron River) was the cut-off point; the Coda-Iron River stub apparently stayed in place for now.
