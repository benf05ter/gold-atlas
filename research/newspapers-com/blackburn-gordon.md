# Blackburn murder, Gordon, Douglas Co WI (Oct 1897): newspapers.com read (2026-10-31)

Job 2 from the coordinator. Three questions: (a) was money found in the house or on the land after the murder; (b) who administered the estate; (c) was the estate land sold, or the house later searched or dug.

Source: newspapers.com (Neb's sign-in), read view-only. Image ids are newspapers.com `/image/<id>/`.

## Short answers

- **(a) Money:** No hoard was found. The killer picked or forced the locks and took the money hidden in the bedding, estimated at $500 to $1,200. He missed only two $1 bills in an envelope. A wallet of bills seen on Blackburn in Gordon a few days earlier was never found. The papers put his fortune at about $75,000. They say most of it was in Stillwater MN and St. Paul banks, not at the house. He also owned lands "scattered throughout northern Wisconsin" and some in Minnesota.
- **(b) Administrator:** L. H. Mead of Shell Lake. The half-brother, John T. Blackburn of Shell Lake, petitioned for him. Judge D. E. Roberts appointed Mead special administrator on Oct 7 1897. Letters of administration followed on Nov 2 1897. No will was found.
- **(c) Land and digging:** I found no administrator's sale notice and no later report of anyone searching or digging at the house, up to 1902 in WI papers. Mead petitioned on Oct 14 1898 for his final account and discharge. The "residue of the estate" was to be "assigned to the heirs". So the land most likely passed to the heirs rather than being sold through probate. The heirs were the half-brother, a nephew in Kansas, nieces and nephews in southern Illinois, and the Lightbody grandchildren. This is a negative from newspapers only. The probate file and the Douglas Co deed books were not checked.

## Site detail useful for the pin

- Legal description: **Section 36, T43N, R11W** ("Section 36-43-11"). Also given as "section 36, town 43, range 11", 160 acres, partly cultivated.
- The place was "ten miles southeast of Gordon on the Totogastic [Totagatic] river". The Superior Times says "ten miles below Gordon station, on the Totogastic Unse river".
- **His wife's grave is under the house.** She was an Indian woman who died c.1893. He built the house over her grave and dug his own grave beside hers. The coffin top was level with the floor and had a sliding glass lid. He asked to be buried there, and the funeral was held "at his home" on Oct 5 1897, burying him beside his wife.
- Layout: a 12-room white house with green shutters and a white picket fence, plus a general store and stables. The barn was 40 rods (~200 m) from the house. A log shed was ~50 ft away, where the axe was found. A path led from the house to the river ford, where the house key was dropped.
- He had a $250 county reward (Oct 11 1897). In Feb 1898, Charles Beauregard of Eau Claire Lake was arrested for the murder.
