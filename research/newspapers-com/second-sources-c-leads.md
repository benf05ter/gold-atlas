## 2. tw-wi-glencoe-roland-1893 (Buffalo Co.)
- Milwaukee Sentinel 1893-11-19 p4, "Murdered for Money: Patrick Roland of Glencoe Dies From His Wounds" (ARCADIA, Wis., Nov 18). This is a separate dispatch from Arcadia. https://www.newspapers.com/image/1297950172/
- NEW FACTS: Patrick Roland, an old soldier "who has lived as a miser in the town of Glencoe, SIX MILES FROM THIS CITY [Arcadia]", was struck on the forehead with an axe by two strangers who came by train to Arcadia on Thursday (Nov 16) and walked to his residence demanding money. He died Nov 18. They "took what money he had on his person, about $50". "Mr. Roland was a man of wealth, but a miser in every sense of the word. NO ONE KNOWS WHERE HIS MONEY IS HIDDEN." The murderers were lurking "in the woods somewhere in the vicinity of his HUT", and "it is known that the murderers have occupied a VACANT SCHOOL HOUSE for several nights". The G.A.R. kept him out of the potter's field. He had lived in the community since the war, and the neighbors suspected relatives.
- Wide coverage Nov 19-29, 1893 (Milwaukee Sentinel 11/21 p10 with 9 matches, Dodge County Citizen 11/23 p1, Superior Times, etc.). The 11/21 and 11/29 follow-ups were not read.
- Placement: Roland's hut in Glencoe twp, about 6 mi from Arcadia (Glencoe twp lies SW of Arcadia), near a then-vacant schoolhouse.

## 3. tw-wi-eaton-swantek-1932 (Brown Co.)

## 21. tw-mn-le-center-tree-gold-1906 (Le Sueur Co.)
- SEPARATE DISPATCH: Minneapolis Tribune 1906-05-19 p1, "Recalls Early Hanging" (Special to the Tribune, LA CROSSE, Wis., May 19; 16 matches): https://www.newspapers.com/image/181157359/
- FACTS: "The mystery surrounding the finding of gold coins in an old tree trunk near LE SUEUR CENTER, Minn., is probably solved" by Alderman WILLIAM GROVER of La Crosse, formerly of Henderson. In 1853 his father entered a homestead 3.5 miles from what is now Henderson ("then an Indian trading post"). Next-door neighbor REINHART lived "3.5 miles the other side of the trading post", at "the edge of the prairie". In spring 1858 a young German with oxen, a wagon and about $600 IN GOLD COIN stayed with the Grovers, then went to Reinhart's place. He "dropped out of existence". In fall 1858 a hunter found part of his remains in the Reinhart clearing. Reinhart confessed and was LYNCHED by settlers (from a 15-mile radius). "After the lynching, the gold belonging to the young German could not be found. The gold just found in Le Sueur county, near Le Sueur Center, WAS JUST ABOUT WHERE REINHART LIVED." The Grovers lived in what is now Sibley Co.
