# The History of Waukesha County (Western Historical Co., 1880): vanished and early sites

Source: archive.org `historyofwaukesh00west`. I read the OCR text view-only and kept it in scratch only.

Page numbers are printed book pages, recovered from the running-head numbers. They were cross-checked against heads quoted in the text, e.g. "787" in the Summit chapter and "701" in the Oconomowoc chapter. Treat them as ±1.

Coverage:
- Town chapters: pp. 729-794.
- Waukesha village chapter: pp. 628-690.
- Oconomowoc city chapter: pp. 690-728.
- Plus site mentions in the biographies, cited as "bio" because their page numbers were not recoverable.

Method: same as the Washington-Ozaukee file. I ran a keyword sweep, then read each hit in context. Gaps are possible.

## Treasure, loot and hoard stories

1. **C. Y. Reed store-safe robbery, Oconomowoc, c.1855 (pp. 700-701).** Burglars took $1,000-1,500 from the safe. A mesmerised "clairvoyant" said it was first hidden in a hollow tree, then moved; a $2 bill was found where he pointed. He then said the money was "sunk in a can to the bottom of the lake". The lake was dragged "to no purpose". A New York detective then claimed a gang of horse thieves and counterfeiters were based there. **The book does not say the money was ever recovered.** The lake is not named; it is presumably La Belle or Fowler at Oconomowoc. This is the strongest lost-loot lead in the three books so far.
2. **Summit Bank robbery attempt, Oconomowoc, 1868 (p. 701).** Unsuccessful.
3. **H. McCoy, builder of the "old barracks" (p. 698).** He fled the country c.1840s-50s, suspected of being "a counterfeiter and general rogue". The barracks stood a little N of the present Oconomowoc mill.
4. **Remington store, Genesee, c.1850 (bio).** Broken into and robbed one night by "a band of (supposed) Mormons". No amount or recovery is given.

No miser's-hoard or buried-cache story was found in the town chapters.
