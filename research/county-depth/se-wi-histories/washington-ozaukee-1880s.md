# History of Washington and Ozaukee Counties (Western Historical Co., 1881): vanished and early sites

Source: archive.org `historyofwashing00west`. I read the OCR text view-only and kept it in scratch only, not saved to the repo. Page numbers are the printed book pages, recovered from the OCR running heads, so treat them as ±1.

Coverage:
- Washington Co town chapters: pp. 388-470.
- Ozaukee Co town chapters: pp. 507-545, plus the Old Settlers' address on pp. 476-484 and 544-548.
- I also used site mentions from the biographical sketches that follow, cited as "bio" because their page numbers were not recoverable from the OCR.

Method: I searched the OCR for keywords (mill, dam, kiln, quarry, brewery, distillery, cheese, ashery, tannery, tavern, post office, platted, hamlet, log school or church). Then I read each hit in context. OCR misses are possible, so a site absent from this list is not proof it is absent from the book.

Format: name | town | sec/landmark | dates (builder/owner) | page

## Treasure, loot and hoard stories

1. **Asa Case robbery, c.1843-44 (p. 508).** Case was the old Port Washington storekeeper. He sold his store and set out on foot for Sheboygan. A peddler found him "about ten miles north of Port Washington, lying on the road" with his throat cut. Case said two men had robbed him of all his money. He refused to eat and starved to death. The place is the old Milwaukee-Sheboygan trail about 10 mi N of Port Washington. The money was never recovered.
2. **Jacob Leno, miser murdered, spring 1853 (pp. 511-512).** Leno was a German widower living alone in a rented house in Port Washington, next door to John Dose. He was "inclined to be miserly", and rumour said he had "large sums of money horded away", which grew to "thousands". He was found with his throat cut. The murderer is thought to have got $300-400. Henry Wist was suspected and indicted, but nothing was proved. The site is Port Washington village; the house location is not given.
3. **1862 Indian scare: money buried in the garden (p. 499).** Ulrich Landolt told of a neighbour who put his money in a cigar box to hide it. He dug a hole in the garden, then covered the empty hole and left the box sitting on the ground. Comic, and the money was recovered. No place or name is given beyond Ozaukee Co.
4. **Mrs. Frances Schaffner (p. 512).** Henry Wist made an attempted night robbery, armed with a rifle, on her "valuables and money" in the Arcade Building, Port Washington, c.1850. It failed.
5. Not treasure: G. H. Young lost $1,000 in gold at the Milwaukee land office in 1845 and got it back (p. 392). "Miserly Joe" Gardinier at Hamilton (p. 531) is only a nickname; the book has no hoard story about him.

None of the four stories describes an unrecovered cache at a findable spot. Case (2-3 mi of trail) and Leno (village lot) are the closest to leads.
